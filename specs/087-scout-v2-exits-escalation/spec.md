# Feature Specification: Scout V2 Escalation Layer, Declared Exits, and an Auditor-Free Turn

**Feature Branch**: `087-scout-v2-exits-escalation`

**Created**: 2026-09-27

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "@docs/kanban/ciclo 13/scoutv2/briefs/05-escalation-exits-desmonte-auditor.md" — Fase 05 of the ScoutV2 epic, executed after Brief 04 (typed instructions, playbook index, mid-turn `open_playbook`) and Brief 11 (trigger-based interruption and the always-on baseline it re-registers). In the existing engine, three things go wrong around how a conversation *ends*. First, the rule "never ask a question when transferring to a human" is written three times — once in the always-on guidelines, once more in a reminder block, and once more inside the stage-transition tool's result — because a rule read many messages earlier is not salient enough on its own. Second, changing an opportunity's stage and transferring to a human are offered to the assistant as tools on every single turn, including a turn where the customer only asked a question or asked to pause. Third, to catch the assistant getting those outcomes wrong, every reply passes through a stack of after-the-fact checks — an action classifier, an independent re-confirmation of that classifier, a claim-consistency check, a repair step, a reclassification and a reverification — costing at least 3 and up to ~8 language-model calls per turn. The re-confirmation exists because the classifier itself hallucinates: a customer picking one of several offered options was misread as "accepted a transfer to a human" 5 out of 5 times; and the repair loop can itself trigger a transfer and send the customer two messages. This feature makes every outcome an explicit, declared action decided and validated by the system, keeps escalation to a human always possible, delivers the transfer-closing rule exactly once, and lets the new engine run with no after-the-fact checks at all.

**Terminology**: An **exit** is an action the assistant takes to declare that the conversation has reached an outcome. There are exactly three kinds: **terminal** (the current procedure is finished), **transition** (the conversation moves on to another named procedure), and **escalation** (the conversation is transferred to a human). A playbook declares its own exits; in addition, one **built-in escalation exit** (`exit_handoff`) exists for every conversation, independently of any playbook. A **turn-ending exit** is a terminal or escalation exit. A **step** is one iteration of the assistant's work inside the turn's single top-level model call (the assistant asks for actions, gets their results, and continues). The **baseline** is the always-available set of actions defined by Brief 11, re-registered whenever an interruption takes over the turn. "Pinned procedure", "situational playbook", "procedure playbook" and "interruption takeover" keep the meanings defined in Brief 11's specification.

A **top-level model call** is the turn's single request-and-reply exchange with the language model that produces the customer's reply (one provider request per step, all sharing one growing transcript). Model calls made inside an action the assistant uses (for example, a classifier an existing tool runs internally) and the fail-safe's existing contact-memory generation (FR-021) are not top-level calls.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-27

- Q: When an escalation exit succeeds (built-in `exit_handoff` or a declared one), what should happen to the conversation's routing state — its active procedure and its "last exit" record? → A: Exactly as for a terminal exit, reusing the existing terminal routing-state operation unchanged: the active procedure is cleared (active playbook, activation time and activation reason become empty) and the exit's name (`handoff` for the built-in exit, the declared name otherwise) replaces any earlier last exit. The cumulative transition count and the per-turn limit marker are untouched. It happens only when the transfer is actually performed, in the same place as the transfer; a refused (FR-022), invalid (FR-008) or stage-check-failed (FR-014) escalation leaves routing state unchanged. The playground records the same change on the sandbox conversation. A conversation handed back by a human is routed from scratch on its current facts.
- Q: After a valid exit's data is checked, where does that data go besides the turn's record of actions (FR-024)? → A: Nowhere persistent — not the opportunity, the contact, or the routing state, and no new column or table. The only exception is escalation: the built-in `exit_handoff`'s transfer note carries the supplied reason; a declared escalation exit's transfer note carries the exit's name followed by each declared field and its value, in declaration order. Terminal and transition exit data goes only to the record of actions. Opportunity data is written only through the opportunity-management tool; a stage move checks the fields as already stored and never fills them from exit data.
- Q: If a declared escalation exit also moves the opportunity's stage and the transfer is refused because the conversation is no longer in the assistant's care (FR-022), does the stage still move? → A: No. An escalation exit is all-or-nothing: data validation (FR-008), then the still-in-the-assistant's-care check (FR-022), then the stage requirements (FR-014); only after all three pass are the stage move, transfer, note and routing-state change written as one unit. If the transfer turns out not to be performed during those writes (the conversation changed hands at that moment), the stage move is undone too. A refused escalation leaves the opportunity, the conversation's team and assignee, the transfer notes and the routing state unchanged; its result tells the assistant neither the transfer nor the stage move happened, and it is recorded in the record of actions like any failed exit.
- Q: On a v2 turn, should an empty final reply be handled like an unreadable final output (the fail-safe transfer), including after a successful terminal or transition exit? → A: Yes. An empty or whitespace-only final reply is treated exactly like an unreadable final output whether or not an exit succeeded: on the inbox, the existing fail-safe transfer (standard localized handoff message, transfer, internal alert note); in the playground, an error and no transfer. A terminal or transition outcome already recorded in that turn stands, and the fail-safe changes routing state no further. The only exception is a turn where an escalation exit succeeded: no fail-safe, no second transfer, no alert note — the standard localized handoff message is sent as the turn's single customer message (in the playground, shown as the reply).

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Escalating to a human is always possible, and the closing rule arrives exactly once (Priority: P1)

Whatever the conversation's state — a procedure pinned, a situational playbook opened mid-turn, or nothing pinned at all — the assistant always has, in its standing instructions, a dedicated escalation section that says when to transfer to a human and what to do before transferring, and it always has the built-in escalation exit available to actually do it. A customer who writes "I want to talk to a person" on a conversation with no active playbook is transferred in that same turn and receives one closing message, without the assistant first having to open any playbook. How to write that closing message (natural, no questions, a human will continue) is told to the assistant exactly once, in the result of the escalation exit — the standing instructions do not repeat it, and a stage change never repeats it.

**Why this priority**: Escalation is the safety valve of the whole engine. The standing instructions already tell the assistant to escalate; without an always-available mechanism, the most literal request a customer can make would depend on two separate guesses by the model (first open a playbook, then escalate), and a customer asking for a human could simply be ignored.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by running a turn on a conversation with no pinned procedure where the customer explicitly asks for a human, and confirming that the escalation section is present in the standing instructions, that the built-in escalation exit is offered, that the conversation ends the turn transferred to the human queue with a transfer note, that the customer receives exactly one message, and that the closing rule appears only in the escalation exit's result.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any v2 turn, **When** the assistant's standing instructions are assembled, **Then** they contain an escalation section stating when to escalate and the procedure to follow before transferring, separate from the four typed instruction sections.
2. **Given** a conversation with no pinned procedure and no playbook opened, **When** the turn's available actions are assembled, **Then** the built-in escalation exit is offered.
3. **Given** a procedure is pinned, or an interruption has taken over the turn, or a transition has moved the turn to another playbook, **When** the available actions for the next step are inspected, **Then** the built-in escalation exit is still offered.
4. **Given** a conversation with no pinned procedure, **When** the customer explicitly asks to speak with a human and the assistant uses the built-in escalation exit, **Then** in that same turn the conversation is transferred to the human queue, an internal transfer note records the reason, the conversation's last exit becomes `handoff`, and the customer receives exactly one message — the assistant's own closing reply.
5. **Given** any v2 turn, **When** the standing instructions are inspected, **Then** they do not contain the instruction on how to write a transferring turn's closing message; **When** the escalation exit succeeds, **Then** its result contains that instruction exactly once.
6. **Given** an exit that moves the opportunity's stage without escalating, **When** it succeeds, **Then** its result does not contain the transfer-closing instruction.
7. **Given** a conversation with a pinned procedure, **When** an escalation exit (built-in or declared) succeeds, **Then** the conversation has no active procedure (no active playbook, activation time or activation reason) and its last exit equals the exit's name, replacing any earlier value; its cumulative transition count and per-turn limit marker are unchanged.
8. **Given** an escalation exit (built-in or declared, with or without a stage move) that is refused because the conversation is no longer in the assistant's care, has invalid data, or fails a declared stage move's checks, **When** it is processed, **Then** the opportunity's stage, the conversation's team and assignee, the conversation's transfer notes, and every routing-state field (active procedure, activation time, activation reason, last exit, transition counters) are identical before and after the attempt.

### User Story 2 - Declared exits are validated by the system before anything happens (Priority: P2)

A playbook declares, in its own file, the exits it can end with: each exit's name, its kind, the data the assistant must supply with it (field names and types), the target procedure for a transition, and optionally the pipeline stage the opportunity moves to. While that playbook is in effect, each of its exits is offered to the assistant as an action. When the assistant uses an exit with data that does not match the declaration, the system rejects it with an error explaining what is wrong, nothing happens, and the assistant corrects itself within the same turn — no extra top-level call, nothing sent to the customer. When the data is valid, the system performs the outcome and hands a short text result back to the assistant, which then writes the customer's final message. A turn that ends without any exit is the normal case: the assistant answered and is waiting for the customer.

**Why this priority**: This is the mechanism that replaces after-the-fact guessing with structure. Every outcome becomes something the assistant explicitly declared and the system checked, so a wrong outcome is stopped before it happens instead of detected afterwards. It depends on User Story 1's escalation layer being the always-available fallback.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested with a fixture catalog containing a procedure that declares one terminal exit, one transition exit, and one escalation exit with typed fields: confirming each exit is offered only while its playbook is in effect, that invalid data is rejected with no effect and a later valid attempt in the same turn succeeds within the same top-level call, that each kind produces its outcome, that every exit result is text followed by a final customer message, and that a turn with no exit records no outcome.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a playbook in effect declares exits, **When** the available actions are assembled, **Then** each declared exit is offered; **Given** that playbook is not in effect, **Then** none of its exits is offered.
2. **Given** the assistant uses a declared exit with a missing field, an undeclared field, or a value of the wrong type, **When** the exit is processed, **Then** it returns an error describing every problem, no outcome is recorded, no data is changed, nothing is sent to the customer, and the turn continues.
3. **Given** the assistant corrects the data and uses the exit again in the same turn, **When** the turn completes, **Then** the outcome happened and the whole turn used exactly one top-level model call.
4. **Given** a valid terminal exit, **When** it is processed, **Then** the conversation no longer has an active procedure and the exit is recorded as the conversation's last exit.
5. **Given** a valid transition exit, **When** it is processed, **Then** the target procedure becomes the conversation's active procedure with activation reason "transition", its full content comes back as the exit's result, and for the rest of the turn the assistant works with the target's guidance, tools and exits instead of the source's.
6. **Given** a valid escalation exit, **When** it is processed, **Then** the conversation is transferred to the human queue as described in User Story 1; for a declared escalation exit the transfer note contains the exit's name followed by every declared field and its value, in declaration order; for the built-in escalation exit it contains the supplied reason.
7. **Given** any valid exit, **When** the turn completes, **Then** the customer received a final message written by the assistant after the exit's result — the turn never ends silently because an exit was used.
8. **Given** a turn in which the assistant uses no exit, **When** the turn completes, **Then** no outcome is recorded and the conversation's active procedure is exactly what routing and playbook opening left it as.
9. **Given** a valid terminal or transition exit with supplied data, **When** it is processed, **Then** the opportunity's and contact's attributes are unchanged apart from any declared stage, and the supplied data appears in the turn's record of actions.
10. **Given** a procedure is pinned, or a procedure playbook was already opened or became a transition target in this turn, **When** the assistant tries to open a procedure playbook, **Then** the opening is refused with an error, nothing changes, and the offered actions stay the same; the procedure changes within a turn only through a transition exit.

### User Story 3 - Outcomes replace the stage-change and transfer tools (Priority: P3)

In the new engine, the assistant is never offered a tool to change an opportunity's stage, a tool to transfer to a human, or a tool to call an arbitrary external API — not on any turn, with or without an active playbook. Changing the stage happens only as part of an exit, and only after the system confirms the opportunity has every field that stage requires. Transferring happens only through an escalation exit. Because an outcome can only come from an exit the assistant explicitly used, nothing the customer writes can, on its own, produce an outcome: the documented failure where a customer picking one of several offered options was read as "accepted a transfer" can no longer happen. The contact-update and opportunity-management tools remain available to playbooks that declare them.

**Why this priority**: Removing the always-offered mutation tools is what makes outcomes single-path — keeping a tool next to an exit for the same effect would leave the choice between them to chance. It builds on User Story 2, which provides the exits that take over these effects.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by assembling the available actions for turns with no playbook, with a pinned procedure, and after an interruption, confirming none of the three removed tools ever appears; by using a stage-moving exit on an opportunity missing required fields and confirming the stage is unchanged and no outcome is recorded; and by replaying a transcript where the customer picks one of several options (with no exit used) and confirming no transfer and no outcome occur.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any v2 turn — no playbook, a pinned procedure, a playbook opened mid-turn, or an interruption takeover — **When** the available actions are assembled at any step, **Then** no stage-change tool, human-transfer tool, or custom-API tool is offered.
2. **Given** a declared exit that moves the opportunity to a stage whose required fields are not all filled, **When** the assistant uses it with valid data, **Then** the exit returns an error naming the missing fields, the stage does not change, and no terminal, transition, or escalation outcome is recorded.
3. **Given** the same exit after the missing fields are filled, **When** the assistant uses it, **Then** the opportunity moves to that stage and the exit's outcome is recorded.
4. **Given** a customer message choosing one of several options the assistant offered, **When** the assistant replies without using any exit, **Then** the conversation is not transferred and no outcome is recorded.
5. **Given** a playbook declares the contact-update tool or the opportunity-management tool, **When** it is in effect, **Then** that tool is offered.

---

### User Story 4 - The new engine runs with no after-the-fact checks (Priority: P4)

A complete turn in the new engine costs exactly one top-level language-model call — against a minimum of 3 and up to ~8 in the existing engine with its reply auditor on. None of the existing after-the-fact components (action classifier, its re-confirmation, claim-consistency check, repair, reclassification, reverification) runs anywhere on the new engine's path. The assistant's final output contains only the message for the customer, with no internal-reasoning field that only ever fed logs. Genuine system failures still fall back to a human: exhausted quota, missing API key, or a final output that cannot be read all lead to the existing fail-safe transfer. Accounts still on the existing engine keep every one of those checks unchanged.

**Why this priority**: This is the payoff of User Stories 1–3 — once outcomes are declared and validated, the stochastic after-the-fact correction has nothing left to compensate, and keeping it would only add cost, latency and a source of double messages. It is last because it is only safe once the structural replacements exist.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by running v2 turns (no exit, a valid exit, an invalid-then-corrected exit) and counting exactly one top-level model call in each with no auditor component invoked; by inspecting the final output's shape; and by simulating exhausted quota, a missing API key, and an unreadable final output, confirming each ends in the fail-safe transfer on the inbox.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any v2 turn, **When** it completes, **Then** exactly one top-level model call was made.
2. **Given** any v2 turn, **When** it completes, **Then** none of the reply-audit components (action classifier, independent re-confirmation, claim-consistency check, repair, reclassification, reverification) was invoked.
3. **Given** any v2 turn, **When** the assistant's final output is produced, **Then** it contains the message for the customer and no internal-reasoning field.
4. **Given** an inbox conversation on a v2 account whose final output cannot be read — including an empty or whitespace-only final reply — **When** the turn completes, **Then** the conversation is transferred through the existing fail-safe (standard handoff message, transfer, internal alert note).
5. **Given** exhausted quota or a missing API key on a v2 account, **When** a turn would run, **Then** no top-level model call is made and the existing fail-safe behavior applies unchanged, including its existing contact-memory generation when the scout has memory enabled.
6. **Given** an account without the new engine, **When** a message is processed, **Then** the existing engine's audit components, tools and prompt behave exactly as before this feature.
7. **Given** an inbox turn in which a terminal exit, or a transition exit, succeeded and the final reply is empty, **When** the turn completes, **Then** the customer received exactly one message — the standard handoff message — the conversation is transferred with the internal alert note, and the exit's routing-state outcome is still in place.
8. **Given** an inbox turn in which an escalation exit succeeded and the final reply is empty, **When** the turn completes, **Then** the customer received exactly one message — the standard handoff message — and the conversation has exactly one transfer note and no alert note.
9. **Given** an inbox turn on a scout with memory enabled, in which no escalation exit succeeded and the final reply is empty, **When** the turn completes, **Then** exactly one top-level model call was made, the fail-safe ran, the only other model call is the fail-safe's existing contact-memory generation, and no reply-audit component was invoked.

### Edge Cases

- The assistant keeps sending invalid data to an exit: every attempt is rejected with no effect; if the turn ends without a valid attempt, no outcome is recorded and the customer receives the assistant's final reply as in any turn without an exit.
- The assistant uses a turn-ending exit and then, later in the same turn, another exit or tool: the later action is rejected with an error and has no effect; the first outcome stands and the assistant writes its final reply (FR-015).
- In one step the assistant asks for several actions, one of them an exit: they are processed in the order listed; actions after a successful turn-ending exit are rejected; actions after a successful transition are checked against the target playbook's actions (FR-016).
- A third transition within the same turn: it is refused, nothing changes, the per-turn limit marker is recorded, and the exit's result tells the assistant the transition did not happen and to reply to the customer now (FR-011).
- A transition's target has no tools and no exits of its own: for the rest of the turn the assistant has only the baseline — that is not an error.
- An exit declares a stage move but the conversation has no opportunity, or the scout has no stage configured for that stage role: the exit returns an error, no stage changes, no outcome is recorded.
- The escalation exit succeeds but the final reply comes back empty or whitespace-only: FR-012 applies (the single exception to FR-021).
- A terminal or transition exit succeeds but the final reply comes back empty or whitespace-only: FR-021 applies — the fail-safe runs and the exit's routing-state outcome stands.
- The escalation exit is used on an inbox conversation that is no longer in the assistant's care (a human already took it over, or it was resolved) between the start of the turn and the exit — including at the very moment the transfer is being written: no stage move (undone if already applied), no transfer, no change to team or assignee, no transfer note, no routing-state change, and the exit's result tells the assistant that neither the transfer nor the stage move happened (FR-022).
- A human hands a previously escalated conversation back to the assistant: the assistant does not resume the procedure that was pinned before the escalation; the next turn is routed from scratch on the conversation's current facts, exactly like a conversation with no active procedure (FR-012).
- An interruption takes over a turn whose pinned procedure declared exits: those exits stop being offered for the rest of the turn (Brief 11 takeover); the built-in escalation exit remains (FR-003).
- A procedure playbook is opened mid-turn with nothing pinned: its declared exits become available from the next step, together with its tools.
- A procedure playbook was opened mid-turn (or one is pinned, or a transition made one the target) and the assistant tries to open a procedure playbook, including the same one again: the opening is refused with an error and nothing changes (FR-007).
- A playbook's body mentions `exit_handoff` without declaring it: this is valid — the built-in escalation exit is available to every playbook without declaration (FR-006).
- The escalation exit is used in the playground: the outcome and its routing-state change (procedure cleared, last exit recorded) are recorded on the sandbox conversation and shown in the playground's list of actions with the closing reply, but the sandbox conversation is not placed in a human queue (FR-023).

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Escalation layer**

- **FR-001**: Every v2 turn's standing instructions MUST include an escalation section, as its own block placed after the playbook index and before any pinned procedure's content, stating (a) when to escalate to a human and (b) the procedure to follow before transferring. The four typed instruction sections (Identity, Communication, Memory, Safety) MUST remain exactly as defined by Brief 04; the escalation section is not a fifth section inside that block.
- **FR-002**: No always-present content of a v2 turn — the typed instruction sections, the playbook index, the escalation section — MUST contain the instruction on how to write the closing message of a transferring turn.
- **FR-003**: The built-in escalation exit MUST be offered on every v2 turn, from the first step, whether or not a procedure is pinned, and MUST remain offered after an interruption takeover and after a transition (it is part of the baseline).
- **FR-004**: The built-in escalation exit MUST require a non-empty reason for the transfer; a missing or empty reason MUST be rejected as invalid data (FR-008).

**Exit declaration and validation**

- **FR-005**: A playbook MUST be able to declare zero or more exits. Each declared exit MUST state: its name; its kind (terminal, transition, or escalation); the data fields the assistant must supply, each with a type; for a transition, the target procedure's name; and, optionally for any kind, the stage role (qualified or unqualified) the opportunity moves to as part of the exit.
- **FR-006**: The playbook catalog's startup validation MUST reject, listing every problem in one pass alongside the existing checks: an unknown exit kind; a transition without a target, with a target that does not exist, that is the declaring playbook itself, or that is a situational playbook; a target on a non-transition exit; a field type outside the supported set (text, integer, number, true/false, date-time); a field name outside the existing name format; a stage role outside the supported set; a playbook declaring an exit named `handoff` (reserved for the built-in escalation exit); a situational playbook declaring any exit; an exit entry that is not a mapping; a `fields` value that is not a mapping; and an unknown key in an exit entry. A body reference to `exit_handoff` MUST be valid without a declaration.
- **FR-007**: A playbook's declared exits MUST be offered exactly while that playbook is in effect in the turn: from the first step when it is the pinned procedure (until an interruption takeover or a transition replaces it), from the next step after it is opened mid-turn, or from the next step after a transition makes it the target. They MUST NOT be offered otherwise. At most one procedure playbook is in effect per turn: while a procedure is pinned, or after a procedure playbook was opened mid-turn or became a transition target in this turn, opening a procedure playbook (including the same one again) MUST be refused with an error that changes nothing; within a turn, the procedure changes only through a transition exit (FR-011).
- **FR-008**: Before any effect, an exit's data MUST be checked against its declaration: every declared field present, no undeclared field, every value of its declared type. Invalid data MUST return an error result describing every problem found, MUST NOT change any data or record any outcome, MUST NOT send anything to the customer, and the turn MUST continue within the same top-level model call.
- **FR-009**: A valid exit MUST perform its outcome and return a text result that lets the assistant continue the turn; an exit MUST NEVER end the turn's step loop itself. The customer's message for the turn is the assistant's final reply, written after the exit's result. An exit's supplied data MUST NOT be stored on the opportunity, the contact, or the conversation's routing state, and no new storage is added for it: it goes to the turn's record of actions (FR-024) and, for an escalation exit only, into the transfer note (FR-012). The only persisted effects of a terminal or transition exit are its routing-state change and, when declared, its stage move.

**Exit outcomes**

- **FR-010**: A valid terminal exit MUST clear the conversation's active procedure and record the exit's name as the conversation's last exit (existing routing-state behavior). Its result MUST tell the assistant the outcome was recorded and to write its final reply.
- **FR-011**: A valid transition exit MUST make the target the conversation's active procedure with activation reason "transition", counted toward the existing limit of two transitions per turn. Its result MUST be the target's full content, and for the rest of the turn the source's guidance, tools and exits MUST be replaced by the baseline plus the target's tools and exits. A third transition in the same turn MUST be refused: nothing changes except the per-turn limit marker being recorded, and the result MUST tell the assistant the transition did not happen and to reply to the customer now.
- **FR-012**: A valid escalation exit (built-in or declared) MUST transfer the conversation to the human queue using the scout's configured transfer settings, record an internal transfer note (with the conversation's opportunity reference, as today), and deliver exactly one customer-facing message for the turn: the assistant's final reply. No separate automated transfer message MUST be sent in addition; only when the final reply is empty or whitespace-only MUST the standard localized handoff message be sent instead — without the FR-021 fail-safe, a second transfer, or an alert note (in the playground, that message is shown as the reply). For the built-in escalation exit, the transfer note MUST carry the supplied reason; for a declared escalation exit, it MUST carry the exit's name followed by each declared field and its value, in the order they are declared. In the same place the transfer is performed, and only when it is actually performed, the escalation MUST change routing state exactly as a terminal exit does (FR-010), reusing that operation unchanged: the active procedure is cleared (active playbook, activation time and activation reason become empty; nothing to clear when none was pinned) and the exit's name — `handoff` for the built-in exit, the declared name for a declared exit — replaces any earlier last exit. The cumulative transition count and the per-turn limit marker MUST NOT change. A conversation later handed back by a human MUST be routed from scratch on its current facts, like any conversation with no active procedure.
- **FR-013**: The result of a successful escalation exit MUST contain, exactly once, the instruction to write a natural closing message with no questions, stating that a human will continue. No other exit result — terminal, transition, or a stage move — MUST contain it.
- **FR-014**: When an exit declares a stage move, the system MUST, before writing anything, confirm that the conversation has an opportunity, that the scout has a stage configured for that stage role, and that every field required to enter that stage is filled (including the scout's qualification requirements for the qualified stage), checking the opportunity's fields as already stored — exit data MUST NEVER fill them; opportunity data is written only through the opportunity-management tool. Any failure MUST return an error naming what is missing and MUST leave the stage and the routing state unchanged, with no outcome recorded. A stage move MUST NEVER, by itself, transfer the conversation; a transfer happens only through an escalation exit.
- **FR-015**: After a turn-ending exit (terminal or escalation) succeeds, any further exit or tool the assistant requests in that turn MUST return an error and have no effect.
- **FR-016**: When one step contains several actions including an exit, they MUST be processed in the order listed. Actions after a successful turn-ending exit follow FR-015; actions after a successful transition MUST be checked against the actions available after it (FR-011), and an action no longer available MUST return an error for that action only.
- **FR-017**: A turn in which no exit succeeds MUST NOT record any outcome — no last exit, no transition, no transfer, no stage move — and MUST NOT infer one from the text written by the customer or the assistant.

**Tool catalog and turn shape**

- **FR-018**: No v2 turn MUST ever offer a stage-change tool, a human-transfer tool, or a custom-API tool, at any step. Playbooks MUST be able to declare the contact-update tool and the opportunity-management tool.
- **FR-019**: Every v2 turn MUST make exactly one top-level language-model call (see Terminology), including turns where an exit was rejected and corrected. No reply-audit component (action classifier, independent re-confirmation, claim-consistency check, repair, reclassification, reverification) MUST be invoked anywhere on the v2 path. The fail-safe's existing contact-memory generation (FR-021) is not a top-level call and is unchanged.
- **FR-020**: The assistant's final output on a v2 turn MUST consist of the message for the customer only, with no internal-reasoning field.
- **FR-021**: On a v2 turn, exhausted quota and a missing API key MUST keep their existing fail-safe behavior. A final output that cannot be read — including a final reply that is empty or whitespace-only, whether or not an exit succeeded earlier in the turn — MUST, on an inbox conversation, trigger the same fail-safe transfer the existing engine uses (standard localized handoff message, transfer, internal alert note and, when the scout has memory enabled, its existing contact-memory generation); in the playground it MUST return an error to the playground and transfer nothing. A terminal or transition outcome already recorded in that turn MUST stand, and the fail-safe MUST NOT change routing state further. A turn in which an escalation exit succeeded is the only exception and follows FR-012 instead.
- **FR-022**: An escalation exit MUST be all-or-nothing, checked in this order before anything is written: its data (FR-008), then that the conversation is still awaiting the assistant (not taken over by a human, not resolved), then any declared stage move's requirements (FR-014). Only when all three pass MUST the stage move, the transfer, the transfer note and the routing-state change (FR-012) be written, as one unit. If any check fails — or the transfer turns out not to be performed while these writes happen because the conversation changed hands at that moment — the opportunity (including its stage, undoing a stage move already applied), the conversation's team and assignee, its transfer notes and its routing state MUST all remain as they were; the exit's result MUST tell the assistant that neither the transfer nor the stage move happened, and the attempt is recorded like any failed exit (FR-024).
- **FR-023**: In the playground, exits MUST validate and record their outcomes against the playground's sandbox conversation exactly as on the inbox — including an escalation's routing-state change (FR-012) — and the escalation exit MUST be reported as a successful outcome in the playground's list of the turn's actions without placing the sandbox conversation in a human queue.
- **FR-024**: Every exit attempt, valid or not, MUST appear in the turn's reviewable record of actions with its name, the data supplied, and its result.
- **FR-025**: This feature MUST NOT change the existing (v1) engine: its audit components, its stage-change and human-transfer tools (including the closing instruction in the stage-change result), and its prompt keep behaving exactly as today for accounts without the new engine.

### Key Entities

- **Exit Declaration**: Part of a playbook file. Name, kind (terminal / transition / escalation), typed data fields, transition target (transition only), optional stage role. Validated at startup together with the rest of the playbook catalog. The supplied field values are a check, not stored data: they reach only the turn's record of actions and, for escalation, the transfer note.
- **Built-in Escalation Exit**: The single escalation exit that exists independently of any playbook, always offered on v2 turns, requiring a transfer reason. Its name is reserved.
- **Escalation Section**: Always-present block of the standing instructions stating when to escalate and what to do before transferring; does not contain the closing rule.
- **Exit Result**: The text handed back to the assistant after an exit is processed — an error describing invalid data, the transition target's content, a confirmation to write the final reply, or, for a successful escalation, the one-time closing instruction.
- **Turn Outcome**: What an exit changes in persisted data: last exit and cleared procedure (terminal), active procedure with reason "transition" and transition counters (transition), transfer to the human queue plus transfer note plus the same last exit and cleared procedure as terminal (escalation), and optionally the opportunity's stage. At most one turn-ending outcome per turn.
- **Stage Role**: The scout-level mapping from a role (qualified, unqualified) to a concrete pipeline stage of that scout's account, letting a shared playbook file name a stage without knowing any account's stage identifiers.
- **Transfer Note**: The internal, non-customer-visible note recorded on a transfer, with the linked opportunity reference as today, plus the supplied reason (built-in escalation exit) or the exit's name followed by each declared field and value in declaration order (declared escalation exit).

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: 100% of v2 turns — with no exit, a valid exit, or a rejected-then-corrected exit — use exactly one top-level language-model call as defined in Terminology (versus a minimum of 3 and up to ~8 on the existing engine with its reply auditor on).
- **SC-002**: In an automated verification where the model's responses are scripted to use the built-in escalation exit after an explicit request for a human on a conversation with no pinned procedure, 100% of those turns end, in that same turn, with the conversation transferred and exactly one message delivered to the customer. Whether a live model chooses the exit for a given wording is observed in the manual playground check, not measured by this criterion.
- **SC-003**: The transfer-closing instruction appears 0 times in the standing instructions of any v2 turn, 0 times in any non-escalation exit result, and exactly once in every successful escalation exit result.
- **SC-004**: 0 outcomes (last exit, transition, transfer, stage move) are recorded on v2 turns in which no exit succeeded, and 0 are recorded by exits rejected for invalid data or missing required fields.
- **SC-005**: 0 v2 turns in which an exit succeeded end without a message delivered to the customer, and 0 escalations deliver more than one automated message to the customer.
- **SC-006**: 0 v2 turns, at any step, offer a stage-change, human-transfer, or custom-API tool.
- **SC-007**: 100% of startup attempts with a malformed exit declaration (per FR-006) fail before any conversation is served, reporting every problem in one pass.
- **SC-008**: Accounts on the existing engine show zero change in the number of model calls, prompt content, tool catalog, or audit behavior.

## Assumptions

- This feature builds on Brief 04 (typed instructions, playbook index, mid-turn opening) and Brief 11 (interruption takeover and the baseline). It adds the built-in escalation exit to Brief 11's baseline, so every takeover re-registers it automatically.
- Brief 04's specification kept exits "as data only" when a playbook is opened; this feature is the one that turns them into offered actions, both for a pinned procedure and for a playbook opened or transitioned to mid-turn.
- The design document's example of an exit declaration lists only field signatures; that declaration shape is extended to also carry the kind, the transition target and the optional stage role (FR-005). The exact file syntax is a planning decision.
- The routing-state behavior of terminal and transition outcomes (clearing the active procedure, last exit, activation reason "transition", the two-per-turn limit and its marker) already exists from Brief 02 and is reused unchanged; this feature is what calls it from a live turn.
- The existing transfer service and stage-change rules are reused as effects. In the existing engine the transfer is performed after the model's reply using that reply as the transfer message; the new engine keeps that one-message outcome (FR-012), but performs the transfer — together with the stage move, note and routing-state change — while the escalation exit is processed (FR-022), so the conversation is already transferred when the final reply (or, if it is empty, the standard handoff message) is delivered afterwards as the turn's single customer message. How that delivery is implemented is a planning decision.
- A v2 escalation performs only the transfer (team, status, transfer note, routing state); unlike the existing engine's transfer, it does not generate contact memory, so no model call happens while the conversation is locked for the transfer. The fail-safe keeps its existing memory generation (FR-021).
- The escalation section's wording is authored in code in Portuguese (like the other standing sections), derived from the existing engine's "fallback to human" guidance, and is the same for every scout; making it configurable per scout is out of scope.
- Moving an opportunity to the qualified stage no longer transfers the conversation automatically on the new engine (FR-014). A playbook that wants the existing engine's "qualified ⇒ transfer" behavior declares an escalation exit with the qualified stage role. Which playbooks declare which exits (including adding exits to the existing qualification playbook) belongs to Brief 06; this feature is verified with fixture playbooks.
- A situational playbook may not declare exits (FR-006): escalation is already available to it through the built-in exit, and a terminal or transition outcome from a playbook that is never persisted as active would have no defined meaning. Brief 06's situational playbooks need no exits of their own.
- There is no "return to the previous playbook" exit kind: returning is structural (an interruption is never persisted, per Brief 11).
- No cap on the number of correction attempts for an invalid exit is added; there is no evidence of a loop risk that would justify one.
- The existing engine's audit components are left in place for accounts without the new engine; removing them physically belongs to the deprecation phase (Brief 10). An out-of-turn safety net for conversations left without an outcome is deferred (design §6.7). The anti-false-promise rule stays in the Safety section, since a promise with no action ("I'll let you know tomorrow") is not covered by any exit.
- Whether a v2 inbox reply is delivered when a human took the conversation over during the turn is existing behavior, unchanged by this feature beyond FR-022.
