# Feature Specification: Scout V2 Capabilities and End-to-End Scheduling

**Feature Branch**: `089-scout-v2-capabilities-scheduling`

**Created**: 2026-09-30

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "@docs/kanban/ciclo 13/scoutv2/briefs/07-capabilities-e-agendamento.md" — Fase 07 of the ScoutV2 epic, executed after Brief 06 (spec `088-scout-v2-first-playbooks`), which shipped the `agendamento` playbook degraded: its two required capabilities (`scheduling`, `customer_registry`) exist only as names in a catalog, are unsatisfied for every account, and the playbook therefore always ends in a transfer to a human. Today the four account-configured HTTP tools that reach the ERP make the **model** produce values that are configuration, not conversation — company, user, agreement, agreement number, person origin, schedule and service identifiers — on a path that writes to the customer's ERP. This feature makes the two capabilities real: a hard-input / soft-output contract, a per-account resolution that says whether an account can satisfy a capability, an ERP-backed satisfaction that injects every configuration value and the contact's phone itself, and a complete `agendamento` playbook that takes a qualified lead from customer lookup to a booked appointment.

**Terminology**: "Playbook", "procedure playbook", "situational playbook", "pinned procedure", "interruption takeover" and "baseline" keep the meanings defined in `specs/085-scout-trigger-interruption`. "Exit", "terminal / transition / escalation exit", "built-in escalation exit" (`exit_handoff`) and "stage role" keep the meanings from `specs/087-scout-v2-exits-escalation`. "Capability", "business fact", "pending step", "unsatisfied items", "completed playbooks" and "activation reason" keep the meanings from `specs/088-scout-v2-first-playbooks`. An **operation** is one callable action a capability offers to the assistant (e.g. "list free slots"); a capability is **satisfied** for an account when some integration of that account can carry out all of its operations, and **unsatisfied** otherwise. **Configuration identifiers** are the seven ERP values that belong to the account's setup rather than to the conversation: company, user, agreement, agreement number, person origin, schedule and service. The **ERP integration** is the account's enabled integration of category ERP (today the single vendor Younus).

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-30

- Q: The rescheduling/cancellation-policy step of `agendamento` has no data source and would stay pending in every turn; how should this feature treat it? → A: Remove the step (the one marked `[[depends: politica_reagendamento]]`) and the `needs: [politica_reagendamento]` declaration, so the playbook is literally without pending steps: with both capabilities satisfied the guidance shows no pending step or notice and the recorded unsatisfied items are empty for `agendamento`; with unsatisfied capabilities they list only `scheduling` and `customer_registry`. No source for the policy text is built. The generic business-fact mechanism from Brief 06 stays as is. The assistant states no rescheduling or cancellation rules during scheduling; if the lead asks, the commercial-question interruption answers only from the knowledge base and transfers when it has no answer. The Brief 06 contract tests that count markers and pendings in the `agendamento` file are updated to the new body: 4 markers, none for `politica_reagendamento`.
- Q: How is "the nearest available date" found when the requested day has no free time, given that the listing operation takes a single date? → A: With the same listing operation, on at most the three days following the requested one, in order; the first day with a free time is the one offered. If none of the three has a free time the assistant closes through the "no slot" exit stating that no availability was found. No new operation and no further search.
- Q: What happens when a required setting is present but malformed (e.g. a non-numeric company or schedule identifier on an already-saved hook)? → A: The capability is unsatisfied, exactly like a missing setting: the company, user, agreement, person-origin, schedule and service identifiers must contain only digits, the agreement number and the credential must be non-blank. A malformed value never reaches a request and never raises.
- Q: What does the optional `service` input of "list free slots" do? → A: It is informational: recorded in the trace, never sent. The ERP service is always the configured one.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - A capability is a strict contract the assistant cannot bend (Priority: P1)

A capability declares its operations, what each one needs from the assistant (named, typed, required or optional inputs) and the shape of what each one returns. When the assistant calls an operation wrongly — a required input missing, or an input of the wrong type — the call fails before anything is sent to the outside world and the assistant is told exactly what to fix, in the same turn, so it can correct itself and try again. When the outside system answers in an unexpected shape, the answer is recorded for the operator and still handed to the assistant: a vendor's variation never breaks the customer's turn. Resolving a capability for an account never raises an error: an account with no suitable integration simply gets "unsatisfied", which is what makes the degradation delivered by Brief 06 work.

**Why this priority**: This is the foundation the other two stories stand on. Everything the assistant is allowed to do on the customer's ERP passes through this contract, so its strictness on the way in — and its leniency on the way out — decides whether the flow is safe and whether it survives real vendor behavior.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested without any ERP by declaring the two capabilities, calling each operation with a missing input, with a wrongly typed input and with valid input against a stand-in for the outside system, confirming the stand-in receives no request for the first two; by having the stand-in return a payload missing a declared output field and confirming the assistant still receives it and the deviation is recorded; and by resolving a capability for an account with no ERP integration and confirming an "unsatisfied" answer instead of an exception.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an operation with a required input, **When** the assistant calls it without that input, **Then** no request leaves the system, the assistant receives an error naming the missing input, and the turn continues so the assistant can call again correctly.
2. **Given** an operation whose input is declared as a date, **When** the assistant passes something that is not a date, **Then** no request leaves the system and the assistant receives an error naming the input and the expected type.
3. **Given** an operation with an optional input, **When** the assistant omits it, **Then** the call proceeds.
4. **Given** a valid call, **When** the outside system returns data missing a field the operation declares as required in its output, **Then** the deviation is recorded in the turn's trace, the data is still returned to the assistant, and the turn completes normally.
5. **Given** an account with no ERP integration, **When** a capability is resolved for it, **Then** the answer is "unsatisfied" and no exception is raised.
6. **Given** an account whose ERP integration is present and fully configured, **When** `scheduling` and `customer_registry` are resolved, **Then** both are satisfied and the routing snapshot for the turn lists them as satisfied.
7. **Given** an account whose ERP integration exists but lacks any configuration identifier one capability needs, **When** that capability is resolved, **Then** it is unsatisfied — not attempted and failed halfway — and the playbooks that require it degrade as delivered by Brief 06.

### User Story 2 - The integration hides configuration from the assistant (Priority: P2)

The assistant sees only what belongs to the conversation: a date, an optional service name, a slot it was offered, notes, a person's name. Every credential and configuration identifier — and the customer's phone — is supplied by the system from the account's ERP integration settings and from the contact record. The four ERP actions the account's HTTP tools perform today (look a person up by phone, register a person, list free slots for a date, create an appointment) become available through the two capabilities, with all identifiers filled in by the integration. An account that has not entered the identifiers gets an unsatisfied capability, never a malformed request to the ERP.

**Why this priority**: It removes the measured hallucination surface — values the model was forced to invent on a path that writes to the customer's ERP — and it is what makes the ERP integration the satisfier of the capabilities. It depends on User Story 1's contract and precedes the end-to-end flow.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested against a stand-in for the ERP: configuring the integration with distinctive values for every identifier, calling each of the four operations with only conversational inputs, and inspecting the requests the stand-in received to confirm every identifier and credential is the configured one and none came from the call; then removing one identifier and confirming the capability resolves as unsatisfied and no request is sent.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the operations of both capabilities, **When** their input declarations are inspected, **Then** none of them asks the assistant for company, user, agreement, agreement number, person origin, schedule or service identifiers, for a credential, or for the customer's phone.
2. **Given** a contact with a phone and a fully configured ERP integration, **When** the assistant looks the customer up, **Then** the request to the ERP carries the contact's phone and the configured company, and no value produced by the assistant.
3. **Given** the customer is not found, **When** the assistant registers the customer with the name the lead gave, **Then** the request carries that name, the contact's phone and the configured company, user, agreement, agreement number and person origin.
4. **Given** the assistant asks for free slots on a date, **When** the request is made, **Then** it carries the date and the configured company, schedule and service.
5. **Given** the assistant books one of the slots it was offered, **When** the request is made, **Then** it carries the slot's start, the identifier of the customer found or registered earlier in this conversation, the notes if any, and the configured company, schedule, service, user and agreement.
6. **Given** the ERP integration's settings lack one identifier that a capability needs, **When** a turn that requires that capability is routed, **Then** the capability is unsatisfied and no request to the ERP is made in the turn.
7. **Given** a contact with no phone on record, **When** the assistant calls a customer-registry operation, **Then** it fails before any request, with an error saying the contact has no phone, and the assistant is not asked for one.
8. **Given** the assistant calls the booking operation with a slot that was never returned by a listing in this conversation, or before any customer was found or registered in this conversation, **When** the call is validated, **Then** it fails before any request, with an error that tells the assistant what is missing.
9. **Given** any capability call, **When** the turn trace is inspected, **Then** it records the operation, the inputs the assistant supplied, the identifiers and phone sent to the ERP, and the outcome — and never the credential.
10. **Given** an account with the ERP integration configured only for the contact-data card (company and token, none of the new identifiers), **When** the contact-data card is used, **Then** it works exactly as before.
11. **Given** a booking already succeeded in this conversation, **When** the assistant calls the booking operation again (with any slot), **Then** it fails before any request, with an error telling the assistant that the appointment already exists and that it must close through the scheduled exit.

### User Story 3 - A qualified lead is scheduled end to end in the playground (Priority: P3)

A lead who has just been qualified — with a phone on record — is moved to the scheduling playbook in the same turn, exactly as Brief 06 delivered. What changes is what happens next: the assistant finds or creates the customer by the contact's phone, asks which day suits, offers no more than three free times in natural language, books the one the lead picks and closes the procedure as scheduled with the booked start. If the requested window has nothing free it offers the nearest available date, and if the lead turns every option down it closes as "no slot" with a reason. A request for a person, a clinical urgency, or two failed attempts to locate the customer end in a transfer. A lead qualified without a phone reaches the same playbook, and the playbook ends in a transfer in that same turn, stating that the phone is missing, without asking for it again and without contacting the ERP. Once the procedure ends — scheduled, no slot or transferred — the lead's next message never re-opens it or offers times again, even after a human hands the conversation back.

**Why this priority**: This is the business outcome of the whole epic phase — a lead that becomes an appointment without a human. It comes last because it composes the contract (Story 1) and the integration (Story 2) with the playbook and routing delivered by Briefs 05 and 06.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested in two ways. First, with the real playbook file, the real routing and a stand-in for the ERP, running scripted multi-turn conversations for the happy path, the no-slot path, the handoff paths and the no-phone path, and checking the turn trace, the recorded exit and the requests the stand-in received. Second, in the playground against the account's real ERP integration, confirming the identifiers in the recorded requests are the configured ones.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a lead with a phone whose qualification completes, **When** the qualified exit succeeds, **Then** scheduling is pinned by transition in that same turn, and the assistant proceeds with scheduling's steps without waiting for the lead's next message.
2. **Given** scheduling is in effect with both capabilities satisfied, **When** the assistant's instructions are assembled, **Then** no step is shown as pending and no pending-steps notice appears, the scheduling exits and the capability operations are offered, and the unsatisfied items recorded for the turn in the conversation state and the turn trace are empty for `agendamento`.
3. **Given** the customer exists in the ERP, **When** scheduling begins, **Then** the assistant finds them by the contact's phone and creates nothing; **and given** the customer does not exist, **Then** the assistant registers them with the name the lead confirmed.
4. **Given** the ERP has more than three free times on the requested day, **When** the assistant presents options, **Then** at most three are presented, in natural language, and none is presented that the ERP did not return.
5. **Given** the lead picks one of the offered times, **When** the assistant books it and uses the scheduled exit, **Then** the appointment exists in the ERP, the turn ends in the scheduled outcome with the booked start and the booking reference recorded, and scheduling is recorded as completed.
6. **Given** the requested day has no free time, **When** the assistant offers the nearest available date and the lead accepts a time on it, **Then** booking proceeds as in scenario 5.
7. **Given** the assistant offered the nearest available date and the lead declines every option, **When** the assistant uses the no-slot exit with a reason, **Then** the turn ends in the no-slot outcome with that reason and scheduling is recorded as completed.
8. **Given** the lead asks for a person, declares a clinical urgency, or the assistant failed twice to locate the customer, **When** the assistant uses the built-in escalation exit, **Then** the conversation is transferred exactly as Brief 05 defines and scheduling is recorded as completed.
9. **Given** the assistant attempts the scheduled exit when no booking succeeded in this conversation, **When** the exit is validated, **Then** it is rejected with an error telling the assistant it has not booked, nothing is recorded, scheduling is not recorded as completed, and the assistant can still book or transfer.
10. **Given** scheduling ended in the scheduled, no-slot or transfer outcome, **When** the lead sends another message in the same conversation — including after a human hands the conversation back — **Then** scheduling is not pinned by state, no time is offered again and no booking is attempted.
11. **Given** a lead qualified with no phone on record (declined or ignored the request in identification), **When** qualification's qualified exit succeeds, **Then** scheduling is pinned by transition, no customer-registry request is made, and in that same turn the assistant uses the built-in escalation exit stating that the phone is missing, without asking the lead for it.
12. **Given** the ERP does not answer, rejects the credentials, or answers with an error while scheduling is in effect, **When** the assistant calls an operation, **Then** it receives a failure result, does not promise or confirm any booking, and transfers through the built-in escalation exit in the same turn.
13. **Given** an account whose capabilities are unsatisfied, **When** a qualified lead reaches scheduling, **Then** the behavior is exactly the Brief 06 degradation: pending steps, its declared exits not offered, the turn ending through the built-in escalation exit, no ERP request, and the unsatisfied items recorded in the conversation state and the turn trace list `scheduling` and `customer_registry` only.
14. **Given** a scripted qualified lead with a phone and a stand-in ERP with free times, **When** the conversation runs in the playground against the account's real ERP integration, **Then** the recorded requests carry the identifiers configured in the integration settings.

### Edge Cases

- The ERP returns several people for the contact's phone: the assistant is told the match is ambiguous, no person is picked silently, and the attempt counts as a failed lookup toward the two-failure transfer.
- The ERP returns a well-formed answer with an unexpected shape (missing or extra fields, a different envelope): the deviation is recorded, the answer still reaches the assistant, and the turn is not broken. Structural rigor applies to what the assistant sends, not to what the vendor returns.
- The assistant passes a date that is syntactically valid but in the past: it is a valid date for the contract; whatever the ERP answers is reported to the assistant as any other result. The contract checks types, not business rules.
- The lead changes the requested day after seeing options: the assistant lists slots again for the new date; slots from earlier listings in this conversation remain bookable, since they were offered.
- The lead chooses a time that was not among the offered ones (e.g. names a different hour): the assistant cannot book it; it lists or offers again from what the ERP returned.
- A booking request fails at the ERP after slots were offered (the time was taken meanwhile): the assistant receives the failure, does not confirm, and offers other times from a fresh listing or transfers.
- A second booking after one succeeded (e.g. the lead changes their mind after the confirmation): rejected before any request; rescheduling is out of scope, so the assistant closes as scheduled, or transfers if the lead insists.
- The ERP confirms a registration or a booking but returns no identifier for it, or returns a single person whose identifier cannot be read: the assistant receives a failed result (outcome unknown, do not repeat) instead of "created", "not found" or a booking, so it neither registers the same person twice nor books twice, and it transfers.
- The scheduled exit declares no fields: its start and reference come from the recorded booking, so a start supplied by the assistant is an undeclared field and is rejected like any other. Whether the exit is accepted depends only on whether a booking succeeded in this conversation, not on the assistant's wording.
- An interruption takeover (`duvida_comercial`, `pausa`, `fora_de_prospeccao`) happens while scheduling is pinned: existing interruption rules apply — capability operations are offered only while scheduling itself is in effect, not during the takeover.
- The lead asks about rescheduling or cancellation rules during scheduling: the playbook has no such step and the assistant states no rules of its own; existing routing applies — the commercial-question interruption answers only from the knowledge base and, with no answer, transfers through the built-in escalation exit.
- The account has the ERP integration disabled between two turns: the next turn's capability resolution reflects it; a conversation mid-scheduling on that account degrades on that turn as any unsatisfied requirement does.
- The account's ERP vendor implements neither capability (any category-ERP vendor other than Younus): both capabilities are unsatisfied for that account.
- Only one of the two capabilities is satisfied (e.g. the customer-registry identifiers are present but the scheduling ones are not): scheduling requires both, so its exits are hidden and the turn degrades as in scenario 13 of Story 3.
- The account still has active HTTP tools configured for the existing engine: they remain untouched for the existing engine and are not offered by ScoutV2.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Capability contract**

- **FR-001**: The system MUST define each capability as a named contract declaring its operations; each operation MUST declare its inputs (name, type, required or optional) and the shape of its output (required fields and whether it returns one record or a list).
- **FR-002**: The system MUST provide exactly two capabilities in this feature: `scheduling`, with operations "list free slots" (inputs: date, required; service, optional; output: a list of slots each with a slot identifier and a start) and "book" (inputs: slot identifier, required; notes, optional; output: an appointment identifier); and `customer_registry`, with operations "find by phone" (no assistant input) and "create" (input: the customer's name, required). The `service` input is informational: it is recorded in the trace and never sent; the ERP service is always the configured one.
- **FR-003**: A call with a missing required input or an input of the wrong type MUST fail before any request to an external system, MUST return to the assistant an error naming the input and the expectation, MUST NOT end the turn, and MUST let the assistant call again in the same turn.
- **FR-004**: An external answer that does not satisfy an operation's declared output shape MUST be recorded in the turn's trace and MUST still be returned to the assistant; it MUST NOT raise an error, fail the turn or trigger the fail-safe transfer. The one exception is a missing identifier that makes success unverifiable (FR-028, FR-029): the operation then returns a failed result instead of the data, still without raising or ending the turn.
- **FR-005**: Capability names in a playbook's requirements MUST be resolved through a per-account registry keyed by capability name; playbooks and integrations MUST NOT reference each other or any account-specific record.
- **FR-006**: Resolving a capability for an account with no integration able to satisfy it MUST return "unsatisfied" without raising, and the routing snapshot of every turn MUST list as satisfied exactly the capabilities that resolve as satisfied for the account at that moment.
- **FR-007**: A capability MUST be satisfied only when the account has an enabled ERP integration of a vendor that implements it and every setting the capability's operations need is present and well-formed (the company, user, agreement, person-origin, schedule and service identifiers contain only digits; the agreement number and the credential are non-blank); otherwise it MUST be unsatisfied. The two capabilities MUST be evaluated independently.

**Integration and configuration**

- **FR-008**: No operation of either capability MUST ask the assistant for company, user, agreement, agreement number, person origin, schedule or service identifiers, for a credential, or for the customer's phone.
- **FR-009**: Every request to the ERP MUST carry the credential and the configuration identifiers from the account's ERP integration settings; the assistant MUST NOT be able to influence them.
- **FR-010**: The ERP integration settings MUST be extendable with the configuration identifiers the two capabilities need — user, agreement, agreement number, person origin, schedule and service (company and credential already exist) — entered through the existing integration settings screen and validated by the same settings-schema mechanism as the existing ones (the five new numeric identifiers accept only digits, or empty when cleared; the agreement number is free text). The new settings MUST be optional so that accounts using the integration only for the contact-data card stay valid and unchanged.
- **FR-011**: The customer's phone in customer-registry operations MUST come from the contact record; when the contact has no phone, the operation MUST fail before any request, with an error stating that the contact has no phone.
- **FR-012**: The ERP integration MUST make the same four ERP actions the account's HTTP tools perform today reachable through the capabilities: look a person up by phone, register a person, list free slots for a date, and create an appointment.
- **FR-013**: The booking operation MUST act for the customer found or registered earlier in this conversation and MUST NOT accept the person's identifier from the assistant; booking with no customer found or registered in this conversation MUST fail before any request.
- **FR-014**: The booking operation MUST accept only a slot identifier returned by a listing in this conversation; any other value MUST fail before any request.
- **FR-028**: Once a booking succeeded in this conversation, the booking operation MUST fail before any request, with an error telling the assistant that the appointment already exists and to close through the scheduled exit; rescheduling is out of scope. A booking answered without an appointment identifier is returned as a failed result (outcome unknown, do not repeat) and is not recorded as a booking: the assistant transfers.
- **FR-015**: An ERP timeout, network failure, credential rejection or error response MUST be returned to the assistant as a failed result, MUST NOT be presented as success, MUST NOT end the turn on its own, and MUST NOT be retried automatically. Except for a credential rejection (definitive: nothing was applied), the failed result MUST tell the assistant that the outcome is unknown (a write that timed out may have been applied) and that it must not repeat the operation. Any such failure ends in an immediate transfer (FR-019).
- **FR-016**: A find-by-phone answer with more than one person MUST be reported to the assistant as ambiguous and MUST NOT select a person. The system keeps no failed-lookup counter: the playbook guidance tells the assistant to count an ambiguous lookup toward the two-failure transfer; a lookup that fails at the ERP is not counted, it transfers at once (FR-015, FR-019). A "not found" answer is not a failed attempt: it leads to registering the customer (the lookup takes no assistant input, so repeating it cannot change its answer).
- **FR-029**: A customer-registry answer that does not allow identifying the person — a registration answered without a person identifier, or a single match whose identifier cannot be read — MUST be returned as a failed result (outcome unknown, do not repeat), never as "created" or "not found", and no customer is recorded. This keeps the assistant from registering the same person twice or booking for an unknown one.
- **FR-017**: Every capability call MUST be recorded in the turn's trace with the operation, the inputs the assistant supplied, the configuration identifiers and phone sent to the ERP, and the outcome; credentials MUST NOT be recorded.

**Playbook and flow**

- **FR-018**: The operations of a playbook's required capabilities MUST be offered to the assistant only while that playbook is in effect and every one of its required capabilities is satisfied, under the same scoping as its other declared tools; no other playbook and no baseline offers them.
- **FR-019**: The `agendamento` playbook MUST guide the assistant, with no step left pending when both capabilities are satisfied, through: finding or creating the customer by the contact's phone; asking the preferred day; presenting at most three free times in natural language; booking the one the lead confirms and closing as scheduled; offering the nearest available date when the window has no time (found by listing free times, with the same operation, on at most the three days following the requested one; when none of them has a free time, closing as "no slot" stating that no availability was found) and closing as "no slot" with a reason if all options are declined; and transferring on a request for a person, a clinical urgency, two failed attempts to locate the customer (FR-016), or any needed action failing or being unavailable — in that same turn, without promising a booking.
- **FR-020**: For a lead with no phone on record, the playbook MUST guide the assistant to transfer in that same turn, stating that the phone is missing, without asking the lead for the phone and without any customer-registry call.
- **FR-021**: The scheduled exit MUST be rejected, with an error to the assistant and no change of state, unless a booking succeeded in this conversation; rejection MUST NOT record scheduling as completed.
- **FR-022**: The scheduled exit MUST record the booked start and the reference of the booking as its outcome: in the exit's recorded result (turn trace and persisted tool call) and in the conversation's capability state.
- **FR-023**: The state conditions, priority, exits' kinds and the completed-playbook mechanism of `agendamento` MUST remain as delivered by Brief 06; the scheduled and "no slot" outcomes are reachable for the first time and MUST be recorded as completed like any terminal outcome, so that after scheduled, "no slot" or a transfer — including after a human hands the conversation back — the lead's next message does not pin scheduling by state, offer times or attempt a booking.
- **FR-024**: The `agendamento` playbook MUST NOT contain the rescheduling/cancellation-policy step nor declare the business fact `politica_reagendamento`, so with both capabilities satisfied its guidance shows no pending step and no pending-steps notice, and the unsatisfied items recorded in the conversation state and the turn trace are empty for it; the assistant MUST NOT state rescheduling or cancellation rules during scheduling. The generic business-fact mechanism from Brief 06 (declaration field, catalog, boot validation, pending rendering, unmet-needs recording) MUST remain unchanged.
- **FR-025**: Accounts whose capabilities are unsatisfied MUST behave exactly as delivered by Brief 06 (pending steps, declared exits not offered, transfer through the built-in escalation exit in the same turn, unsatisfied items recorded (listing only `scheduling` and `customer_registry`), no ERP request).
- **FR-026**: The account-configured HTTP tools MUST NOT be offered by ScoutV2 and MUST NOT act as providers of capabilities; the existing engine, its tools and prompts MUST remain unchanged, and the ERP contact-data card MUST keep working unchanged.
- **FR-027**: The catalog of capability names MUST stay the single place startup validation checks a playbook's requirements against, so a playbook requiring an unknown capability is still rejected at startup.

### Key Entities

- **Capability**: A named contract the assistant can use (`scheduling`, `customer_registry`), made of operations; satisfied or unsatisfied per account at each turn.
- **Operation**: One action of a capability, with typed named inputs (required or optional) and a declared output shape (required fields; single record or list).
- **Capability Registry**: The per-account resolver from a capability name to "satisfied" (with something that carries out its operations) or "unsatisfied".
- **ERP Integration Settings**: The account's ERP hook settings — credential and company already present; user, agreement, agreement number, person origin, schedule and service added as optional values.
- **Configuration Identifiers**: The seven ERP values that describe the account's setup; supplied by the system, never by the assistant.
- **Resolved Customer**: The ERP person found or created earlier in the conversation, kept so booking can act for them without the assistant supplying an identifier.
- **Offered Slots**: The slots returned by listings in the conversation; the only ones booking accepts.
- **Booking**: The appointment created in the ERP by a successful booking call; its existence is the precondition of the scheduled exit and its reference and start are the exit's outcome.
- **Capability Call Record**: Per-call entry in the turn trace: operation, assistant inputs, injected identifiers and phone, outcome, and any output-shape deviation — without credentials.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: 100% of calls with a missing required input or a wrongly typed input produce a correctable error to the assistant with 0 requests to the outside system, and the same turn accepts the corrected call.
- **SC-002**: 0 operations across both capabilities ask the assistant for any of the seven configuration identifiers, a credential or the customer's phone; 100% of requests recorded in verification carry the configured identifiers and none produced by the assistant.
- **SC-003**: 100% of accounts missing, or holding a malformed value for, any identifier a capability needs, or having no ERP integration or an unsupported vendor, resolve that capability as unsatisfied, and 100% of scheduling turns on those accounts degrade as Brief 06 defines with 0 requests to the ERP.
- **SC-004**: In scripted verification (scripted model turns against a stand-in ERP), a qualified lead with a phone reaches the scheduled outcome, with an appointment created, within four lead messages after the message that completed qualification. The scripted run proves the wiring; the assistant's own choices are confirmed in the playground pass (SC-010).
- **SC-005**: Structurally, 100% of scheduled outcomes are preceded by a successful booking in the same conversation, 100% of scheduled-exit attempts without a successful booking are rejected, and 100% of booking calls with a slot not returned by a listing in the conversation are rejected. For model-driven behavior — at most three time options, presented in natural language, none absent from the ERP's answer — the playbook guidance MUST state the rule and the playground pass (SC-010) MUST show 0 turns violating it; the system does not filter the assistant's wording.
- **SC-006**: 100% of leads qualified without a phone end in a transfer in the same turn, stating the missing phone, with 0 customer-registry requests and 0 requests for the phone.
- **SC-007**: After scheduled, "no slot" or a transfer, 0 subsequent messages in that conversation — including after a hand-back — pin scheduling, offer times or attempt a booking.
- **SC-008**: 100% of turns in which the ERP returns an answer outside the declared output shape complete normally, with the deviation visible in the trace.
- **SC-009**: 100% of ERP failures during scheduling (timeout, credential rejection, error response) produce a turn with 0 booking promises or confirmations and a transfer in the same turn.
- **SC-010**: The scenarios of all three user stories are verified in the playground against a real ERP integration, with the identifiers in the recorded requests matching the configured ones; the existing engine and the ERP contact-data card show zero change in behavior.
- **SC-011**: 100% of turns with `agendamento` in effect and both capabilities satisfied show 0 pending steps and record 0 unsatisfied items for it; 100% of such turns on accounts with unsatisfied capabilities record exactly the unsatisfied capabilities and no business fact.

## Assumptions

- Briefs 04, 05, 06 and 11 (specs 085, 087, 088) are delivered first; this feature uses playbook loading and validation, the routing snapshot's capability satisfaction list, pending-step rendering, exit hiding on unsatisfied requirements, terminal-exit recording, completed playbooks and the built-in escalation exit unchanged.
- The policy step and its business-fact declaration are removed from `agendamento` per Clarifications; the business-fact catalog entry `politica_reagendamento` and the generic mechanism stay in place, unused by any shipped playbook; how a policy becomes available to the assistant is a separate decision.
- The ERP integration's settings do not today hold the identifiers the brief expects them to hold (only credential and company exist, and additional properties are rejected), so extending them is part of this feature (FR-010), as optional values.
- Only one vendor, Younus, satisfies the capabilities. Other ERP vendors, and any non-ERP provider (including account HTTP tools as capability providers), are out of scope; the registry exists so they can be added later without touching playbooks.
- An account has at most one ERP integration (the existing integration allows a single hook per account), so resolution needs no tie-breaking between integrations.
- The contact used by the playground conversation carries the phone the scenarios need; running the playground against a real ERP integration performs real writes (customer registration and appointment) in that ERP, so verification uses a test company or agenda.
- The optional `service` input is part of the contract; with this vendor the service identifier is the configured one, and the input is informational only (recorded in the trace, never sent), so it cannot let the assistant choose an ERP identifier. The exact field lists of the scheduled exit's outcome (the brief names an opportunity and a start; this spec records the booked start and the booking reference), and the exact wording of guidance and errors are planning decisions.
- `exit_agendado` is checked against bookings in the conversation, not against the model's claim; this assertion replaces the after-the-fact consistency check of the existing engine on this path.
- Playbook guidance stays in Portuguese and outside the application's translation files; customer replies follow the detected language as today.
- The form labels of the new ERP settings are plain English strings in the integration definition, like the existing `API Token` and `Company ID` labels: the settings form renders them verbatim and offers no translation mechanism, so no `en`/`pt-BR` file changes (justified deviation, recorded in plan.md Complexity Tracking and accepted by the user in-session on 2026-09-30).
- Younus accounts are assumed to operate in Brasília time (UTC-03:00, no DST): slot start times returned by the ERP and the booking start sent to it carry that fixed offset. Other timezones are out of scope.
- Out of scope: account HTTP tools as capability providers and the split between fixed parameters and assistant inputs; any ERP vendor besides Younus; changes to the ERP adapter factory or the ERP controller beyond what category-based resolution already does; blocking validation of the vendor's answers; rescheduling or cancelling appointments; sourcing business facts; any change to the existing engine.
