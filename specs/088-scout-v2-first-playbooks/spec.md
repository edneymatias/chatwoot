# Feature Specification: Scout V2 First-Cut Playbooks and Degradation on Missing Capabilities

**Feature Branch**: `088-scout-v2-first-playbooks`

**Created**: 2026-09-27

**Status**: Planned (plan.md, tasks.md ready; analysis remediated 2026-09-28, third pass applied)

**Input**: User description: "@docs/kanban/ciclo 13/scoutv2/briefs/06-playbooks-primeiro-corte.md" — Fase 06 of the ScoutV2 epic, executed after Brief 04 (playbook index, mid-turn opening), Brief 11 (trigger-based interruption that never persists) and Brief 05 (declared exits, built-in escalation exit, no after-the-fact checks). In the existing engine, five situational rules live in the always-on prompt even though each matters only in one situation: how to handle a contact whose name is a system placeholder or who has no phone; how to respect a lead who asked to pause; how to recognize a contact who is not a new sales lead (an existing customer, a complaint); when to consult the knowledge base for product and price questions; and the ~2,150-character funnel guidance that only matters during qualification. Each was added to fix one simulated conversation and each competes with all the others on every turn. This feature moves those rules into six scoped playbooks — each carrying only the tools and outcomes its situation needs — gives the qualification playbook explicit outcomes, and delivers a scheduling playbook whose integrations do not exist yet, proving that a playbook with missing integrations degrades visibly instead of failing or inventing results.

**Terminology**: "Playbook", "procedure playbook" (activates by conversation state), "situational playbook" (never activates by state; opened by the assistant, never persisted), "pinned procedure", "interruption takeover" and "baseline" keep the meanings defined in Brief 11's specification (`specs/085-scout-trigger-interruption`). "Exit", "terminal / transition / escalation exit", "built-in escalation exit" (`exit_handoff`) and "stage role" keep the meanings defined in Brief 05's specification (`specs/087-scout-v2-exits-escalation`). A **capability** is a named external integration a playbook depends on (here: `scheduling`, `customer_registry`); a **business fact** is a named piece of company policy a playbook depends on (here: `politica_reagendamento`). A capability or business fact is **unsatisfied** when the system has no working source for it; in this feature every declared capability and business fact is unsatisfied by construction. A **pending step** is guidance in a playbook that depends on something unsatisfied and is therefore shown to the assistant as not currently possible. The **turn trace** is the per-turn observability record (playbook, activation reason, unsatisfied items) the engine already produces.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-27

- Q: Once identification has been attempted and the lead declined or ignored a question, should `identificacao` be pinned again on later turns while the name/phone is still missing? → A: No. Identification runs at most once per conversation, defined by "attempted", not by "declined". `contact_identity_incomplete` stays a pure contact-data predicate. A new column `completed_playbooks` (jsonb string array, default `[]`, one migration) is added to `ichatr_scout_conversation_states`; the exit handler appends the declaring playbook's name whenever that playbook leaves through its own transition or terminal exit (a successful escalation also appends — see the last clarification of this session and FR-026). `completed_playbooks` is added to the routing snapshot, and a predicate `playbook_not_completed(<name>)` is true when the name is not in that list. `identificacao` declares `when_state: [contact_identity_incomplete, playbook_not_completed(identificacao)]`. Its body tells the assistant to ask for each missing item once (name first, then phone, never both in one message) and to use its transition exit to `qualificacao` after each missing item has been asked once — whether the lead answered, declined or ignored it — without waiting for an answer. This keeps the v1 rule "never ask again in this conversation, even if the visitor didn't respond". A new conversation gets a new state row, so identification can run again there.
- Q: What kind of exit is `qualificado`? → A: A transition exit to `agendamento` that also moves the opportunity to the qualified stage role. The qualified-stage checks (including the scout's qualification requirements) run first; if they fail, the exit returns an error, the stage does not change and `qualificacao` stays pinned. If they pass, the stage moves and `agendamento` is pinned with activation reason "transition" in the same turn. Pinning by transition does not require `agendamento`'s state conditions, so it covers leads without a phone. With `agendamento`'s capabilities unsatisfied in this feature, its dependent steps show as pending, its declared exits are not offered, and the assistant ends the turn through the built-in `exit_handoff` — a lead who qualifies is transferred in the same turn, as today. The path `identificacao` → `qualificacao` → `agendamento` within one turn uses two transitions and fits the per-turn limit; `exit_handoff` is an escalation and does not count toward it. `qualificacao` is added to `completed_playbooks` like any procedure that leaves through a transition.
- Q: When a contact's name and phone are both on record but the conversation has no open opportunity, how should that conversation get into qualification? → A: `qualificacao` is pinned by state through a new registered condition `in_qualification`, true when the conversation has no active opportunity, or when its active opportunity is open and has pending required fields (the second branch reuses the existing opportunity-open and pending-required-fields conditions). `qualificacao` declares `when_state: [in_qualification, playbook_not_completed(qualificacao)]`; the guard stops a re-pin after `desqualificado` (the opportunity stays open in the unqualified stage — a human review queue — and would otherwise match again next turn). `identificacao` keeps its higher priority, so an incomplete identity is still collected first. With no active opportunity, the pending fields shown to the assistant are the scout's qualification requirements. The body tells the assistant to create the opportunity with the opportunity-management tool as soon as the lead shows commercial interest (the v1 "Intenção Comercial" rule) and never for a conversation without commercial interest; the tool's existing continuity resolution still decides between reusing the contact's open opportunity and creating a new one. The `qualificacao` trigger text drops "com uma oportunidade em aberto". No opportunity is created automatically by the system, and FR-021 is unchanged.
- Q: When a turn's routing pins `qualificacao`, then `qualificado` transitions to `agendamento` mid-turn, and the turn ends in `exit_handoff` (which clears the pinned procedure), which playbook's unsatisfied items should the conversation state and turn trace record? → A: Those of the last procedure in effect during the turn — whichever came last of the procedure pinned at turn start, a procedure opened through playbook opening, or a transition target (here `agendamento`). A situational takeover does not change which procedure that is (situational playbooks are never persisted). The unsatisfied items are computed when the turn completes and written in the same form to both the conversation's routing state and the turn trace, including turns that end in `exit_handoff` or in the fail-safe transfer. The record names the procedure it belongs to and holds its pending fields, unsatisfied capabilities and unsatisfied business facts. An exit that clears the pinned procedure does not clear or skip this record. The trace's playbook and activation reason still describe the routing decision made at turn start. If no procedure was in effect at any point in the turn, the record names no procedure and lists nothing.
- Q: After a qualified lead with a phone is transferred and a human later hands the conversation back to the assistant, should the lead's next message re-pin `agendamento` by state — which, with its capabilities unsatisfied, can only end in another immediate transfer? → A: No. A successful escalation exit also marks a procedure completed, so a hand-back never re-pins a procedure the human already took over (this supersedes "escalation exits do not append" in the first answer above). It covers the built-in `exit_handoff` and any declared escalation exit, including one reached after an interruption took over the turn. The procedure marked is the last one in effect in the turn: pinned at turn start, opened through playbook opening, or reached as a transition target. For transition and terminal exits, the playbook that leaves is still the one added. The infrastructure fail-safe transfer is not an exit and adds nothing; neither do a rejected exit, a refused transition, an interruption opening or preemption. FR-027 is unchanged: a hand-back does not clear the completed list. `agendamento` gets the same guard as the other procedures: `when_state: [opportunity_in_stage(qualified), contact_has_phone, playbook_not_completed(agendamento)]`, so every procedure in the catalog carries a "playbook not completed" condition. After a hand-back, the assistant takes up a finished procedure again only by opening it through playbook opening while no procedure is pinned, when the lead brings the subject back.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Identification and qualification close the state-driven path (Priority: P1)

A contact whose name is a system-generated placeholder, or who has no phone on record, is recognized from conversation state and handled by the identification playbook — at most once per conversation. The assistant asks how the person wants to be called and for a contact phone (never both in the same message, name first), each missing item exactly once, records each answer on the contact, and — as soon as every missing item has been asked once, whether the lead answered, declined or ignored it — moves the conversation on to qualification through a transition, without waiting and without the customer repeating anything. Identification is then never pinned again in that conversation, even if the name is still a placeholder or the phone is still missing. A contact whose identity is complete goes straight to qualification by state — also when the conversation has no active opportunity yet, in which case the assistant creates the opportunity once the lead shows commercial interest. Qualification ends with an explicit outcome: the lead is qualified (after the system confirms every required field is filled, the opportunity moves to the qualified stage and the conversation moves on to scheduling in the same turn), disqualified (the opportunity moves to the unqualified stage), or handed to a human; after any of these outcomes, qualification is never pinned again in that conversation. At every point, the assistant is offered only the tools the current playbook declares: identification can update the contact but cannot touch the opportunity; qualification can manage the opportunity but cannot update the contact.

**Why this priority**: This is the core sales path — most conversations spend most of their lifetime here. It replaces the always-on identity warnings and the funnel guidance with scoped playbooks and turns the end of qualification from "reply and wait" into a declared outcome, which is what makes the rest of the engine (outcome-driven routing, scheduling) reachable.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested with the real playbook catalog by running a scripted multi-turn conversation on a contact with a placeholder name and no phone and an open opportunity with pending qualification fields: confirming identification is pinned by state, that only the contact-update tool (plus the baseline) is offered, that answers are recorded, that the transition pins qualification and marks identification as completed, that later turns keep qualification pinned even with identity still incomplete, that only the opportunity-management tool (plus the baseline) is offered there, and that each of the qualified / disqualified / handoff outcomes produces its effect; and by a second conversation on a contact with a real name and a phone and no active opportunity, confirming qualification is pinned by state on the first turn and is not pinned again after a disqualified outcome.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a conversation whose contact has a placeholder name or no phone, and identification has not completed in this conversation, **When** a turn is routed, **Then** the identification playbook is pinned by state (activation reason "state").
2. **Given** identification is in effect, **When** the available tools are inspected, **Then** the contact-update tool is offered and the opportunity-management tool is not.
3. **Given** identification is in effect and the contact has both a placeholder name and no phone, **When** the assistant's instructions for the turn are assembled, **Then** they list the name and the phone as missing, name first, and tell the assistant to ask for the name only in this message and for the phone in a later one — never both in one message.
4. **Given** the customer supplies their name and phone during identification, **When** the assistant records them and uses identification's transition exit, **Then** the contact's name and phone are updated, qualification becomes the pinned procedure with activation reason "transition", identification is recorded as completed in the conversation, and for the rest of that turn the assistant works with qualification's guidance, tools and exits.
5. **Given** identification is in effect, **When** its instructions are inspected and the assistant uses the transition exit while an item is still missing (the lead declined or ignored it), **Then** the instructions tell the assistant to ask for each missing item once, never ask again in this conversation, and use the transition exit as soon as each missing item has been asked once without waiting for an answer; and the exit succeeds — qualification becomes the pinned procedure — even though the contact's name or phone is still missing.
6. **Given** identification has left through its transition exit and the name is still a placeholder or the phone is still missing, **When** any later turn in the same conversation is routed, **Then** qualification stays pinned and identification is never pinned again in that conversation.
7. **Given** a new conversation with the same incomplete contact, **When** its first turn is routed, **Then** identification is pinned again (completion is per conversation).
8. **Given** qualification is in effect, **When** the available tools are inspected, **Then** the opportunity-management tool is offered and the contact-update tool is not.
9. **Given** qualification is in effect and every required field is filled, **When** the assistant uses the qualified exit, **Then** the opportunity is in the scout's qualified stage, scheduling is the pinned procedure with activation reason "transition", qualification is recorded as completed, and the same turn ends in a transfer to a human — with or without a phone on record — that records scheduling as completed too.
10. **Given** qualification is in effect and a required field is still empty, **When** the assistant uses the qualified exit, **Then** the exit is rejected naming the missing field, the stage does not change, qualification stays pinned, and nothing is recorded as completed.
11. **Given** qualification is in effect, **When** the assistant uses the disqualified exit with a reason, **Then** the opportunity moves to the scout's unqualified stage, the outcome is recorded and qualification is recorded as completed.
12. **Given** qualification is in effect, **When** the assistant uses the built-in escalation exit, **Then** the conversation is transferred to a human exactly as defined by Brief 05 and qualification is recorded as completed.
13. **Given** a contact with a real name and a phone on record, **When** a turn is routed, **Then** the identification playbook is not pinned.
14. **Given** a contact with incomplete identity, a conversation with no pinned procedure, and an open opportunity whose required fields are all filled, **When** identification transitions to qualification and qualification's qualified exit succeeds in the same turn, **Then** both transitions are accepted (within the per-turn limit of two), and the turn ends in a transfer through the built-in escalation exit, which does not count toward that limit; identification, qualification and scheduling are all recorded as completed.
15. **Given** a contact with a real name and a phone, a conversation with no active opportunity, and no qualification exit yet in that conversation, **When** the first turn is routed, **Then** qualification is pinned by state (activation reason "state"), the opportunity-management tool is offered, and the pending fields shown to the assistant are the scout's qualification requirements.
16. **Given** qualification is pinned and the conversation has no active opportunity, **When** the assistant's instructions are inspected and the assistant uses the opportunity-management tool to create an opportunity, **Then** the instructions tell it to create the opportunity as soon as the lead shows commercial interest, and the tool creates it (or reuses the contact's open opportunity, as its continuity resolution decides).
17. **Given** qualification is pinned and the conversation has no active opportunity, **When** a turn completes without the assistant using the opportunity-management tool, **Then** the conversation still has no opportunity (the system never creates one on its own), and qualification's instructions forbid creating one for a conversation without commercial interest.
18. **Given** qualification left through the disqualified exit and the opportunity is still open in the unqualified stage with pending required fields, **When** any later turn in the same conversation is routed, **Then** qualification is not pinned again.

### User Story 2 - Three situational playbooks cover what state cannot see (Priority: P2)

Some situations have no signature in the database: a contact who is an existing customer or has a complaint, a question about product or price, a lead asking to pause. These are handled by three situational playbooks the assistant opens itself when the situation appears — including in the middle of identification, qualification or scheduling. The out-of-scope playbook ends in a transfer to a human, with no data-changing tool available on the way. The commercial-question playbook answers from the knowledge base and, on the next turn, the procedure that was in progress resumes at the same point. The pause playbook acknowledges politely, does not push pending questions, and changes nothing. None of the three is ever pinned by conversation state, opening one never changes the conversation's pinned procedure, and each opening shows up in the turn trace as opened by the assistant.

**Why this priority**: These replace three always-on rules (pause, out-of-scope intent, knowledge-base usage) whose presence on every turn caused interference; scoping them removes that interference and, above all, removes data-changing tools from turns where the customer only asked a question or asked to pause. It depends on User Story 1's procedures to demonstrate resumption.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested with the real catalog by pinning qualification by state, scripting the assistant to open each situational playbook mid-turn, and confirming the offered tools, the outcome (transfer for out-of-scope, none for the other two), that the pinned procedure is unchanged afterwards and resumes on the next turn with the same pending fields, and that routing never pins any of the three for any combination of conversation state.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** identification, qualification or scheduling is pinned, **When** the customer reveals being an existing customer or files a complaint and the assistant opens the out-of-scope playbook, **Then** for the rest of the turn no contact-update, opportunity-management or other data-changing tool is offered, the conversation ends the turn transferred through the built-in escalation exit, and the procedure that was pinned is recorded as completed.
2. **Given** qualification is pinned with pending fields, **When** the customer asks about a product or price and the assistant opens the commercial-question playbook, **Then** the knowledge-base search remains available, no data-changing tool is offered, and the playbook content returned by the opening tells the assistant to answer only from what the knowledge base returns and never to invent information absent from it.
3. **Given** the previous scenario, **When** the next turn is routed, **Then** qualification is still the pinned procedure, with the same pending fields and the same activation reason as before the question.
4. **Given** any procedure is pinned, **When** the customer signals a pause and the assistant opens the pause playbook, **Then** no data-changing tool is offered, no outcome is recorded, and the playbook content returned by the opening tells the assistant to acknowledge politely and not to reintroduce pending qualification questions in that turn.
5. **Given** any of the three situational playbooks is opened, **When** the turn trace and the conversation's stored procedure are inspected, **Then** the trace records the opened playbook as opened by the assistant (activation reason "trigger") and the stored procedure is unchanged.
6. **Given** any combination of conversation state (contact identity complete or not, opportunity present or absent, in any stage, with or without pending fields, phone present or absent, any set of completed playbooks), **When** a turn is routed, **Then** none of the three situational playbooks is pinned.
7. **Given** the playbook index shown to the assistant, **When** the commercial-question and out-of-scope entries are compared, **Then** each states the situation it covers and excludes the other's (a complaining existing customer is not a commercial question).

### User Story 3 - Scheduling degrades visibly while its integrations are missing (Priority: P3)

A lead who qualifies is moved to the scheduling playbook by qualification's transition in the same turn — with or without a phone on record — and a qualified lead with a phone who comes back later, in a conversation where scheduling has not completed, is routed to it by state, even though the scheduling and customer-registry integrations do not exist yet. Instead of failing or improvising, the playbook shows the assistant which of its steps are not currently possible and why; the assistant never offers time slots and never confirms an appointment, and the only outcome available is a transfer to a human — so a lead who qualifies is transferred in the same turn, as the existing engine does today. That transfer records scheduling as completed, so when a human hands the conversation back, scheduling is not pinned again and the lead is not bounced straight back to the queue. What is missing is recorded on the conversation and in the turn trace, so an operator can see exactly why the conversation was transferred. When the integrations exist (Brief 07), the same playbook file runs its full path unchanged.

**Why this priority**: Scheduling is a new capability, not a migrated rule, and cannot complete until Brief 07. Its value here is proving that degradation works before any integration exists; it is last because the other two stories deliver the migrated behavior.

**Independent Test**: Can be fully tested by putting a contact with a phone and an opportunity in the qualified stage through a turn: confirming scheduling is pinned by state, that the instructions mark the integration-dependent steps as pending, that its declared exits are not offered while its requirements are unsatisfied (the built-in escalation exit is), and that the conversation state and the turn trace list the unsatisfied capabilities and business fact. That the same file runs its full path once its requirements are satisfied is a design property (FR-019), not a scenario a user can reach in this feature.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a contact with a phone, an opportunity in the scout's qualified stage, and scheduling not completed in this conversation, **When** a turn is routed, **Then** the scheduling playbook is pinned by state.
2. **Given** qualification's qualified exit succeeds for a contact with no phone on record, **When** the rest of the turn runs, **Then** scheduling is the pinned procedure with activation reason "transition", its pending steps are shown, and the turn ends in a transfer through the built-in escalation exit.
3. **Given** no procedure is pinned and the contact has no phone, or the opportunity is in any stage other than qualified, **When** a turn is routed, **Then** the scheduling playbook is not pinned by state.
4. **Given** scheduling is in effect with its capabilities unsatisfied, **When** the assistant's instructions are assembled, **Then** they present the steps that depend on the missing capabilities and business fact as pending, naming what is missing, and the turn completes normally.
5. **Given** the same situation, **When** the available actions are inspected, **Then** the scheduling playbook's own exits (scheduled, no slot) are not offered and the built-in escalation exit is.
6. **Given** the same situation, **When** the turn completes, **Then** the conversation's stored unsatisfied items and the turn trace both list `scheduling`, `customer_registry` and `politica_reagendamento`.
7. **Given** the same situation, **When** the assistant's instructions and available actions are inspected, **Then** no tool or exit that offers, books or confirms a time slot is available, the slot-related steps are presented as pending with the instruction not to simulate, promise or confirm them, and the only outcome offered is the built-in escalation exit.
8. **Given** `qualificacao` is pinned by state and every required field is filled, **When** the assistant uses `qualificado` and then the built-in escalation exit in the same turn, **Then** when the turn completes the conversation's routing state and the turn trace both attribute `scheduling`, `customer_registry` and `politica_reagendamento` to `agendamento`, the pinned procedure is cleared, the last exit is `handoff`, both `qualificacao` and `agendamento` are recorded as completed, and the trace's playbook and activation reason still describe `qualificacao` pinned by state.
9. **Given** a qualified lead with a phone was transferred through the built-in escalation exit while scheduling was in effect, and a human hands the conversation back, **When** the lead sends "obrigado", **Then** that turn does not pin scheduling by state, does not transfer the conversation again, and the conversation has no pinned procedure.

### Edge Cases

- The lead declines or ignores the name or phone question during identification: the assistant does not ask again, and once each missing item has been asked once it uses the transition exit to qualification without waiting. Identification is recorded as completed and is never pinned again in that conversation, even though the identity is still incomplete.
- A procedure preempted by a higher-priority state match is not recorded as completed, so it can be pinned again later in the same conversation (e.g. a human removes the contact's phone while `qualificacao` is pinned and identification has not completed in the conversation: `identificacao` preempts it, and `qualificacao` is not recorded as completed). `identificacao` itself is never preempted: it has the highest priority among procedures, and situational playbooks never pin by state. If the conversation is escalated while identification is in effect, identification is recorded as completed and is not pinned again after a hand-back.
- An exit is rejected (invalid data, failed stage checks) or a third transition in the turn is refused: the declaring playbook is not recorded as completed.
- A human hands an escalated conversation back to the assistant: the completed list is kept (escalation clears the pinned procedure, not the completed list) and includes the procedure that was in effect at the transfer, so routing from scratch pins none of the procedures already completed; a qualified lead with a phone is routed to scheduling by state only if scheduling has not completed in that conversation.
- After a hand-back, the lead brings a finished procedure's subject back (e.g. asks again about scheduling): routing does not pin it by state; it comes back only when the assistant opens it through playbook opening while no procedure is pinned, or as the target of a transition from the procedure in effect. If it then leaves or escalates again, its name is kept in the completed list once.
- Identification's transition exit is used on a conversation with no opportunity: qualification becomes the pinned procedure by transition (it would also match by state) and creates the opportunity through the opportunity-management tool once the lead shows commercial interest.
- Identification and qualification (or scheduling) both match by state: the higher-priority playbook wins; identification outranks scheduling and qualification, so identity is collected first — only while identification has not completed in the conversation.
- Qualification is pinned but its pending fields are all filled: it stays pinned (routing keeps a pinned procedure while no higher-priority playbook matches) and the assistant ends it with the qualified exit.
- An active opportunity is open with every required field filled, is not in the qualified stage, and no procedure is pinned (e.g. a human linked an already-complete opportunity to the conversation): no procedure matches by state (`in_qualification` is false and `agendamento` needs the qualified stage). `qualificacao`'s index entry covers qualification data that is collected but not yet confirmed, so the assistant opens `qualificacao` through playbook opening and ends it with the qualified exit once the lead confirms.
- Qualification is pinned by state with no active opportunity and the lead shows no commercial interest (e.g. only a greeting): no opportunity is created and qualification stays pinned; if the contact turns out not to be a new sales lead, the assistant opens `fora_de_prospeccao`.
- Qualification completed (qualified or disqualified) and a human later moves the opportunity back to a stage with pending fields and hands the conversation back: qualification is not pinned again by state in that conversation.
- The qualified exit's stage checks fail (no opportunity, no qualified stage configured, missing required field or qualification requirement): Brief 05 FR-014 applies — error, the stage does not change, no transition happens, qualification stays pinned and is not recorded as completed.
- The customer asks a commercial question and the knowledge base has nothing relevant (or the scout has no knowledge source): the assistant does not invent an answer; it says it doesn't have that information and, if the customer needs it, may transfer through the built-in escalation exit.
- The customer asks a commercial question in the middle of identification: identification resumes on the next turn, with the same missing items.
- The assistant opens the pause playbook while nothing is pinned: nothing is pinned afterwards either.
- A situational playbook is opened on a turn that then transfers through the built-in escalation exit: Brief 05's routing-state change applies (pinned procedure cleared), since the transfer — not the interruption — ends it; the procedure that was in effect before the takeover is recorded as completed, and nothing is recorded when no procedure was in effect during the turn.
- The scheduling playbook is pinned and the customer reveals a complaint: the out-of-scope playbook takes over the turn and transfers to a human; scheduling's pending steps are no longer shown for the rest of the turn, and scheduling is recorded as completed.
- A turn ends in the infrastructure fail-safe transfer (unreadable or empty final output): it is not an exit, so no playbook is recorded as completed and the pinned procedure is left as it was; after a hand-back that procedure is still pinned, unless a higher-priority state match replaces it.
- The opportunity leaves the qualified stage while scheduling is pinned (e.g. a human moves it): scheduling stays pinned until an exit or a higher-priority state match replaces it — existing routing behavior, unchanged.
- A new playbook file references a capability or business fact that is not in the declared catalog: startup validation rejects it (existing capability check extended to business facts).

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Catalog of the first cut**

- **FR-001**: The playbook catalog MUST contain exactly six playbooks: `fora_de_prospeccao`, `identificacao`, `qualificacao`, `agendamento`, `duvida_comercial` and `pausa`, each loaded and validated at startup like the existing `qualificacao`.
- **FR-002**: Each playbook MUST declare exactly the tools, requirements and exits in this table (the built-in escalation exit is available to all of them without declaration, per Brief 05; "none" is literal — no data-changing tool):

  | Playbook | Activation | Declared tools | Requires | Needs | Declared exits |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `fora_de_prospeccao` | situational (no state condition) | none | none | none | none (ends via built-in escalation exit) |
  | `identificacao` | contact identity incomplete + identification not completed in this conversation | contact update | none | none | `identificado` (transition → `qualificacao`) |
  | `qualificacao` | in qualification (no active opportunity, or active opportunity open with pending required fields) + qualification not completed in this conversation | opportunity management | none | none | `qualificado` (transition → `agendamento`, qualified stage role), `desqualificado` (terminal, unqualified stage role) |
  | `agendamento` | opportunity in qualified stage + contact has phone + scheduling not completed in this conversation | none | `scheduling`, `customer_registry` | `politica_reagendamento` | `agendado`, `sem_horario` (terminal) |
  | `duvida_comercial` | situational | none | none | none | none |
  | `pausa` | situational | none | none | none | none |

- **FR-003**: Priorities MUST order the playbooks as: `fora_de_prospeccao` highest of all; among procedure playbooks, `identificacao` above `agendamento` above `qualificacao`. All priorities remain unique, per existing validation.
- **FR-004**: Playbooks in the catalog MUST NOT declare the private-note tool; the transfer note (Brief 05) and end-of-conversation contact summary keep being written by the system.
- **FR-005**: Every playbook's index entry MUST describe the situation it covers; the entries for `duvida_comercial` and `fora_de_prospeccao` MUST each explicitly exclude the other's situation (an existing customer complaining is not a commercial question; a product/price question from a prospective lead is not out of scope).

**New state conditions**

- **FR-006**: A "contact identity incomplete" condition MUST hold when the conversation's contact has a system-generated placeholder name (the same placeholder detection the existing engine uses) or has no phone number on record. It MUST read contact data only.
- **FR-007**: An "opportunity in stage" condition MUST take a stage role as argument (qualified, unqualified, initial) and hold when the conversation's active opportunity is in the scout's stage configured for that role.
- **FR-008**: A "contact has phone" condition MUST hold when the conversation's contact has a phone number on record.
- **FR-009**: A "playbook not completed" condition MUST take a playbook name as argument and hold when that name is not in the conversation's completed playbooks (FR-025).
- **FR-009a**: Playbook files MUST be able to reference the five new conditions in their state conditions (with their arguments), and startup validation MUST reject an unknown condition name, a missing argument for a condition that takes one, an argument given to a condition that takes none, an invalid stage-role argument, or a "playbook not completed" argument that does not name a procedure playbook (one with state conditions) in the catalog — situational playbooks are never recorded as completed (FR-026), so naming one would make the condition always hold.
- **FR-009b**: An "in qualification" condition MUST hold when the conversation has no active opportunity, or when its active opportunity is open and has pending required fields (the second branch is exactly the existing opportunity-open and pending-required-fields conditions combined). When the conversation has no active opportunity, the pending fields shown to the assistant MUST be the scout's qualification requirements.

**Playbook behavior**

- **FR-010**: `identificacao` MUST instruct the assistant to: avoid addressing the contact by a placeholder name; ask for each missing item exactly once — how the person wants to be called first, then a contact phone, never both in one message; record each answer with the contact-update tool when received; never ask again in this conversation, even if the lead did not respond; and use its transition exit to `qualificacao` as soon as each missing item has been asked once, whether the lead answered, declined or ignored it, without waiting for an answer.
- **FR-011**: `qualificacao` MUST carry the funnel guidance that today is always on, minus what the exits now make structural: ask only about the configured pending fields, one at a time, and never invent extra questions to look thorough or because of knowledge-base content; record each answer immediately and do not re-ask recorded data; acknowledge extra information the lead volunteers briefly, without opening a new line of questioning; treat the unqualified stage as a human review queue, never as closing the deal; never move an opportunity that reached the qualified stage backwards. It MUST end with an outcome: `qualificado` once no required field is pending and the lead has confirmed the collected data and wants to proceed (v1: "confirmação com todos os dados"), `desqualificado` on refusal or when the lead does not fit (with the reason), or the built-in escalation exit when a human is needed. The existing guidance about the stage-change and transfer tools does not carry over (those tools do not exist on v2, per Brief 05).
- **FR-011a**: The `qualificado` exit MUST run the qualified-stage checks (including the scout's qualification requirements) before anything changes. If they fail, it MUST return an error, leave the stage unchanged and keep `qualificacao` pinned. If they pass, the opportunity MUST move to the qualified stage and `agendamento` MUST become the pinned procedure with activation reason "transition" in the same turn, regardless of `agendamento`'s state conditions (so a lead without a phone is covered), and the exit's result MUST carry `agendamento`'s rendered guidance. While offering slots is unavailable, that guidance MUST instruct the assistant to transfer through the built-in escalation exit in that same turn (FR-015), and `qualificacao` MUST instruct the assistant to follow the guidance `qualificado` returns in that same turn; its declared exits are not offered (FR-017), so the transfer is the only outcome available. The system guarantees the instruction and the available actions; that the model calls the escalation exit is model behavior, verified by scripted examples (SC-006) and the manual playground pass (SC-007).
- **FR-011b**: `qualificacao` MUST instruct the assistant, when the conversation has no active opportunity, to create one with the opportunity-management tool as soon as the lead shows commercial interest (the v1 "Intenção Comercial" rule) and never to create one for a conversation without commercial interest; the tool's existing continuity resolution decides between reusing the contact's open opportunity and creating a new one. The system MUST NOT create an opportunity automatically. The `qualificacao` index entry MUST NOT require an open opportunity.
- **FR-012**: `fora_de_prospeccao` MUST instruct the assistant to recognize a contact who is not seeking a new commercial opportunity (an existing customer with a product or service in progress, a request to change or cancel something existing, a complaint, or a purely informational question with no purchase interest), not to try to solve it, and to transfer immediately through the built-in escalation exit; a new request of low complexity, preventive or recurring, remains a commercial opportunity and is excluded.
- **FR-013**: `duvida_comercial` MUST instruct the assistant to search the knowledge base for product, service, price and policy questions and answer only from what it returns, never inventing information absent from it; when nothing relevant is found, say so, and transfer through the built-in escalation exit if the customer needs that answer.
- **FR-014**: `pausa` MUST instruct the assistant, when the lead signals a pause or a temporary end ("I'll think about it", "I'll come back later", "thanks"), to acknowledge politely, leave the door open, and not reintroduce pending qualification questions in that turn.
- **FR-015**: `agendamento` MUST describe the full scheduling path (find or create the customer record by phone, ask for the preferred day, offer at most three slots, book the chosen slot and end with `agendado`; offer the next available date when the window has no slot and end with `sem_horario` if all are refused; transfer through the built-in escalation exit when the customer asks for a person, declares a clinical urgency — the scheduling path targets appointment-based businesses such as clinics, as Brief 07 does — or after two failed attempts to find or create the customer record), with each step that depends on a capability or business fact identifying which one. It MUST also tell the assistant that, when offering slots is unavailable, it must not start scheduling, must tell the customer an attendant will continue, and must transfer through the built-in escalation exit in that same turn — so a qualified lead is transferred in the qualifying turn, as the existing engine does today.

**Degradation on unsatisfied requirements**

- **FR-016**: When a pinned or opened playbook has unsatisfied capabilities or business facts, the assistant's instructions MUST present every step that depends on them as pending, naming what is missing, and MUST tell the assistant not to simulate, promise or confirm the pending action; the turn MUST complete normally.
- **FR-017**: While any of a playbook's required capabilities is unsatisfied, that playbook's declared exits MUST NOT be offered; the built-in escalation exit MUST remain offered.
- **FR-018**: When a turn completes — including a turn that ends in the built-in escalation exit or in the fail-safe transfer for an unreadable or empty final output — the system MUST compute the unsatisfied items of the last procedure in effect during that turn (whichever came last of: the procedure pinned at turn start, a procedure opened through playbook opening, a transition target; a situational takeover does not change it) and write the same record to both the conversation's routing state and that turn's trace. The record MUST name that procedure and hold its pending fields (existing content), unsatisfied capabilities and unsatisfied business facts; each list is empty when nothing is unsatisfied. An exit that clears the pinned procedure MUST NOT clear or skip this record. If no procedure was in effect at any point in the turn, the record MUST name no procedure and list nothing. A turn that stops before the model is called (quota exhausted, missing API key) writes no record. The trace's playbook and activation reason keep describing the routing decision made at turn start. This supersedes the 085/087 behavior in which a completed model turn with no successful exit (including a refused exit, or a turn with no procedure in effect) left the conversation's routing state untouched or absent: every completed model turn now writes the record — creating the routing-state row when none exists — and changes no other routing-state field.
- **FR-019**: In this feature, every declared capability and business fact MUST be unsatisfied; making one satisfied MUST require no change to the playbook file — the same file then presents no pending step, offers its exits, and records no unsatisfied item for it. Because no production source can satisfy a capability or business fact before Brief 07, the satisfied path is a design property, not a user-reachable acceptance scenario: it is verified on the components that consume satisfaction, fed a routing snapshot whose satisfaction lists are filled and the unmodified shipped playbook file, and gains an acceptance scenario when Brief 07 delivers a satisfaction source.
- **FR-020**: Startup validation MUST reject a business fact name that is not in the declared catalog of business facts, as it already does for capabilities.

**Tool scoping and resumption**

- **FR-021**: At every step of a v2 turn, the data-changing tools offered MUST be exactly those declared by the playbook in effect (none when no playbook is in effect or a situational playbook has taken over the turn); baseline actions (knowledge-base search, playbook opening, built-in escalation exit) are unaffected.
- **FR-022**: Opening `fora_de_prospeccao`, `duvida_comercial` or `pausa` MUST NOT change the conversation's pinned procedure or its progress; the next turn MUST be routed to the procedure pinned before the opening, as defined by Brief 11.
- **FR-023**: Routing MUST never pin `fora_de_prospeccao`, `duvida_comercial` or `pausa` by conversation state.
- **FR-024**: This feature MUST NOT change the existing (v1) engine's prompt, tools or behavior; the v1 text these playbooks replace stays in place until the deprecation phase (Brief 10).

**Completed procedures** — FR-025–FR-027 and FR-028c are the rule; FR-028, FR-028a and FR-028b state its consequence for each shipped procedure and are kept as explicit acceptance anchors, not as separate mechanisms.

- **FR-025**: The conversation's routing state MUST keep a list of completed playbook names, empty for every new conversation. A new conversation starts with an empty list even for the same contact.
- **FR-026**: A name MUST be added to the completed list, at most once per name and together with the exit's routing-state change, in exactly these cases: (a) when a playbook leaves through one of its own declared transition or terminal exits and that exit succeeds, the playbook that leaves is added; (b) when an escalation exit succeeds — the built-in escalation exit (also when it is used after an interruption took over the turn) or a procedure's declared escalation exit — the last procedure in effect in the turn (whichever came last of: pinned at turn start, opened through playbook opening, reached as a transition target) is added, and nothing is added when no procedure was in effect during the turn. Nothing MUST be added on the infrastructure fail-safe transfer (which is not an exit), a rejected or failed exit, a refused transition, an interruption opening, or preemption by a higher-priority state match.
- **FR-027**: The completed list MUST be part of the conversation facts routing evaluates each turn, and MUST NOT be cleared by an escalation or by a human handing the conversation back.
- **FR-028**: After `identificacao` completes in a conversation, routing MUST never pin it again in that conversation by state, even while the contact's name is still a placeholder or the phone is still missing; the procedure pinned by its transition (or later by state) stays pinned.
- **FR-028a**: After `qualificacao` completes in a conversation (qualified or disqualified), routing MUST never pin it again in that conversation by state, even while the opportunity is open in the unqualified stage with pending required fields.
- **FR-028b**: After `agendamento` completes in a conversation — including by an escalation while it was in effect — routing MUST never pin it again in that conversation by state, even while the opportunity is in the qualified stage and the contact has a phone; a human handing the conversation back MUST NOT cause an automatic re-transfer through `agendamento`.
- **FR-028c**: Every procedure playbook in the catalog (`identificacao`, `qualificacao`, `agendamento`) MUST declare a "playbook not completed" condition naming itself. After a hand-back, a completed procedure MUST NOT become the procedure in effect by state; it becomes the procedure in effect again only when the assistant opens it through playbook opening while no procedure is pinned, or when the procedure in effect transitions into it (a transition ignores the target's state conditions, per Brief 05).
- **FR-029**: In the playground, the completed list MUST be recorded on the sandbox conversation exactly as on the inbox.

### Key Entities

- **Playbook (first-cut catalog)**: One file per situation, with name, title, priority, optional state conditions, index description, declared tools, required capabilities, needed business facts, declared exits and step-by-step guidance written in Portuguese.
- **State Condition**: A named, reusable test on conversation facts; five are added (contact identity incomplete, opportunity in stage with a stage-role argument, contact has phone, playbook not completed with a playbook-name argument, in qualification).
- **Completed Playbooks**: Per-conversation list of procedure playbook names, maintained as FR-025–FR-027 define and read by routing through FR-009/FR-028c.
- **Capability**: A named external integration a playbook requires (`scheduling`, `customer_registry`); satisfied or unsatisfied per turn; unsatisfied for every scout in this feature.
- **Business Fact**: A named company-policy item a playbook needs (`politica_reagendamento`); declared in a catalog; unsatisfied in this feature.
- **Pending Step**: A playbook step shown to the assistant as not currently possible, naming the missing capability or business fact.
- **Unsatisfied Items**: Per-turn record, written identically to the conversation's routing state and the turn trace when the turn completes, of what the last procedure in effect during the turn is missing — the procedure's name, its pending fields (existing), unsatisfied capabilities and unsatisfied business facts. Survives an exit that clears the pinned procedure; names no procedure and lists nothing when no procedure was in effect during the turn.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: In scripted multi-turn verifications, 100% of conversations starting with a placeholder name and no phone reach qualification through identification's transition — including when the transition is used with the name or phone still missing — and identification is pinned 0 times after that in the same conversation; 100% of identification turns carry the ask-once, name-first, never-both instructions.
- **SC-002**: 0 steps across all verified turns offer a data-changing tool not declared by the playbook in effect; in particular 0 steps of `fora_de_prospeccao`, `duvida_comercial`, `pausa` or `agendamento` offer any data-changing tool.
- **SC-003**: 100% of turns following a `duvida_comercial` or `pausa` opening are routed to the same procedure, with the same pending fields, as before the opening.
- **SC-004**: Across every combination of the state facts the conditions read, routing pins `fora_de_prospeccao`, `duvida_comercial` or `pausa` 0 times.
- **SC-005**: 100% of turns with `agendamento` as the last procedure in effect and its capabilities unsatisfied complete without error, show every capability-dependent step as pending, offer none of its own exits, and record the three unsatisfied items attributed to `agendamento` in both the conversation state and the trace — including turns that end in a transfer; 0 such turns offer any tool or exit that offers, books or confirms a time slot.
- **SC-006**: 100% of qualified exits attempted with a required field missing are rejected with no stage change and no transition; 100% of successful qualified exits leave the opportunity in the qualified stage with scheduling pinned by transition, and in scripted verification end the same turn in a transfer, with or without a phone on record.
- **SC-007**: All six playbooks are verified in the playground, each showing the expected activation reason (state, transition or assistant-opened) in its turn trace.
- **SC-008**: The existing engine shows zero change in prompt content, tools or model calls.
- **SC-009**: 100% of scripted conversations with a real-name, phone-bearing contact and no active opportunity get `qualificacao` pinned by state on the first turn with the opportunity-management tool offered; 0 conversations pin `qualificacao` again after a `desqualificado` exit.
- **SC-010**: 0 scripted hand-backs of a conversation escalated while a procedure was in effect re-pin that procedure by state; in particular, 0 hand-backs of a qualified lead with a phone transferred from `agendamento` produce a transfer on the lead's next message without the assistant using an escalation exit.

## Assumptions

- Brief 05 (spec 087) is delivered first; this feature uses its exit declarations, stage roles (qualified / unqualified), stage-requirement checks, built-in escalation exit and routing-state changes unchanged.
- Brief 05 reserves the exit name `handoff` for the built-in escalation exit and forbids situational playbooks from declaring exits. The brief's "`handoff`" outcome for `fora_de_prospeccao`, `qualificacao` and `agendamento` is therefore the built-in escalation exit, referenced in their guidance, not a declared exit.
- `desqualificado` is a terminal exit with the unqualified stage role and a required reason field; `agendado` carries the booked start time and `sem_horario` a reason, both terminal and without a stage role (no "scheduled" stage role exists). Exact field lists are a planning decision.
- `qualificado` is a transition to `agendamento` with the qualified stage role (Clarifications). The qualified-lead transfer the existing engine performs automatically on the stage move therefore happens in the same turn through `agendamento`'s degraded path and the built-in escalation exit. The chain `identificacao` → `qualificacao` → `agendamento` in one turn uses the two transitions the per-turn limit allows.
- The completed list is the only new stored field (one new column on the existing conversation-state table, empty by default); existing rows need no backfill.
- `agendamento` declares no tools: its data changes come from capabilities (Brief 07). The design document's example listing `update_contact` for it is superseded by the Fase 1 table.
- Business facts have no source yet; the declared business-fact catalog holds only `politica_reagendamento`, unsatisfied for every scout. How a business fact becomes satisfied is out of scope.
- Hiding a playbook's declared exits while a required capability is unsatisfied (FR-017) applies to all its exits, not per exit: in the first cut, the only playbook with requirements has only capability-dependent exits.
- Playbook guidance is written in Portuguese (instruction to the model, not customer-facing text) and stays outside the application's translation files; customer replies follow the detected language as today.
- The knowledge-base search is part of the baseline whenever the scout has knowledge available (existing behavior); `duvida_comercial` does not declare it.
- The routing-state copy of the unsatisfied-items record (FR-018) has no reader inside this feature. It is the conversation-side audit Brief 10 US3 requires ("toda conversa em produção explica a si mesma": which playbook was active, why, the outcome and the pending items, readable from the conversation even after an exit cleared the active playbook — design §4.9); the trace copy covers the same need in Langfuse.
- Capability adapters and the capability registry (Brief 07), per-vertical playbook packs, storing playbooks in the database with enable/disable UI, and removing the migrated rules from the existing engine (Brief 10) are out of scope.
